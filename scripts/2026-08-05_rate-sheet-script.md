# The Rate Sheet Script for Wednesday, August 5, 2026

Good morning team. Here is your Wednesday, August 5, 2026 Rate Sheet.

The market move is this: mortgage pricing has improved a little, but the bigger story is that the market is trying to cool down after a rate spike, not that it has fully recovered. Mortgage News Daily's daily survey still shows the average 30-year fixed at 6.75%, down 7 basis points from yesterday's survey, and early market pricing has the 10-year Treasury hovering around 4.61%. Bonds are getting help from lower oil prices and renewed hope that Middle East supply risk could ease, but yields are still sitting high enough that lenders have not been given permission to get generous.

Why it matters is simple. Mortgage rates do not need a perfect economy to improve. They need inflation pressure and rate-hike fear to back off. This morning, bonds are acting like some of that fear came out of the market, but not enough to declare victory. Yesterday's JOLTS report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics showed job openings were little changed at 7.4 million in June, hires held at 5.3 million, and quits held at 3.2 million. In plain English, the labor market is not rolling over fast enough to make the Fed relax.

What this means in plain English: the market went from panic to cautious relief. That is useful. It means the conversation with borrowers can shift from "everything is getting worse by the hour" to "we finally have a window to make a disciplined decision." For newer LOs, remember this: a small bond rally can help tone and pricing even when the headline mortgage rate does not look dramatically different. For experienced LOs, the sharper framing is this: the market is trading a softer inflation scare, not pricing a clean easing cycle.

What to say to customers: "Rates look a little better than they did at the end of last week, but we are still in a market where one strong data print can push costs back up. So the goal today is to use the calmer window wisely, not assume the storm is over."

Lock-or-float guidance: for closings inside 15 days, lock. For closings inside 30 days, the bias is still lock, especially with ADP, ISM services, a 10-year Treasury auction, and Friday's jobs report all crowding the calendar. For 45 days and beyond, selective floating can still make sense if the borrower has room in the payment and you are watching the market actively, but this is not a blind-float environment. If the borrower cannot absorb worse pricing, that is your answer.

Key events and times: today, Wednesday, August 5, ADP private payrolls are scheduled for 8:15 a.m. Eastern, ISM services is due at 10:00 a.m. Eastern, the 10-year note auction is on the Treasury schedule for today, and Governor Lisa Cook is scheduled to speak at 4:05 p.m. Eastern according to the Federal Reserve calendar. Tomorrow, Thursday, August 6, watch jobless claims at 8:30 a.m. Eastern and the 30-year bond auction. Friday, August 7, is still the headline event with the July jobs report at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.

Market and housing context: housing is still giving buyers more room than the rate headlines suggest. Realtor.com's July housing report, published August 4, said median list prices were down 2.4% from a year ago, active listings were up 2.1%, pending listings were up 1.3% but losing momentum, and 20% of listings had a price cut. That is a very useful combination for our business. It says affordability is still strained, but sellers are not holding all the cards. On the mortgage demand side, Fannie Mae's weekly application data published August 4 showed purchase application dollar volume down 2.5% week over week and refinance dollar volume down 3.6% week over week for the week ending July 31. Buyers are still payment-sensitive, and refis are still waiting for a cleaner opening.

Competitive context for online quote objections: when a borrower says, "I found something lower online," the right response is not to argue about the headline. It is to reframe the comparison. "That may be a real ad, but the real question is whether it is your real loan. Online quotes often leave out points, lock timing, mortgage insurance differences, or cash-to-close assumptions. What matters is the rate you can actually execute, not the one designed to win a click." In this market, execution beats advertising.

Pipeline play, with the required refi angle: today is a strong day to work two lists. First, buyers who paused after last week's spike because they assumed the market had become impossible. Second, past clients with FHA, VA, or higher-coupon conventional loans who are not ready for a payment-drop refinance today, but should be lined up for one later. The refi play is readiness, not prediction. "Let's update income, assets, and value now so when the market gives us an opening, we can move in hours instead of days." That is how purchase follow-up becomes future refi inventory.

One rookie mistake to avoid: do not tell borrowers that rates are "coming back down" as if relief is guaranteed. That language creates false hope and turns one bad market afternoon into a trust problem. Say what is true: today is better, but still fragile.

One advanced angle: teach the difference between Treasury improvement and mortgage-rate improvement. Veteran LOs can use this immediately. Even when the 10-year rallies, mortgage rates do not always improve one-for-one because mortgage spreads and lender defensiveness matter. That lets you sound smarter than the LO who only repeats the 10-year quote. The practical borrower version is: "The bond market helps set direction, but mortgage pricing also reflects risk and lender appetite, so improvements can come through unevenly."

Borrower talk tracks: "Today feels better than last week, but better is not the same thing as easy." "High rates are hurting demand, which can improve your leverage with the seller." "If the home and payment make sense now, we can protect the deal and stay ready to improve the financing later." "You do not need perfect rates to make a good decision. You need a safe payment and a smart plan."

One concrete borrower example: imagine a move-up buyer who got spooked by last week's rate jump and started waiting for a big drop. Your script is: "I would not call this a big drop. I would call it a more usable market. Sellers are cutting prices in more listings, and rates are a little better than they were a few days ago. That may be enough to structure a deal that works today, especially if we negotiate concessions and keep a refinance plan in our back pocket." That keeps urgency honest and practical.

One thing to watch: whether today's 10:00 a.m. Eastern ISM services data and the 10-year auction support the bond rally or interrupt it. If services come in hot or the auction is weak, lenders can turn defensive again fast. If the data cools and the auction is absorbed well, the market can keep leaning into the calmer tone.

Coaching close for the team: your edge today is not predicting rates. It is translating uncertainty into a plan. If you can explain why the market feels a little better, why buyers still have negotiating power, and why a purchase conversation today can become a refinance win later, you will sound like an advisor instead of a rate repeater.
