# The Rate Sheet Script

Monday, July 20, 2026

## Market move

Good morning. The bond market is starting the week a touch weaker, which means mortgage rates are best described as stable to slightly worse at the open, not sharply higher, but not getting extra help yet either. After Friday's improvement, the 30-year fixed market is still living in the mid-6s, with Mortgage News Daily's daily index at 6.63% as of Friday, and today's early MBS read says lender pricing impact should be minimal unless bonds slip further during the day.

## Why it matters

What matters this morning is not a huge rate spike. It is that last week's relief rally has not turned into a clean all-clear. The 10-year Treasury has pushed back up into the mid-4.5% range, and the market is still balancing two competing stories: cooler inflation data helped rates late last week, but higher oil and fresh geopolitical risk are keeping a floor under bond yields today.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: rates got a little breathing room late last week, but they are not in a true downtrend yet. If a borrower asks whether the market is getting materially better, the honest answer is not yet. It is better than the worst levels from earlier last week, but still elevated enough that payment sensitivity is real, especially for first-time buyers and cash-tight move-up buyers.

## What to say to customers

Here's the borrower-friendly version: "The market improved a bit at the end of last week, but this morning it's basically flat to slightly worse. So the opportunity isn't that rates are suddenly cheap. The opportunity is that we may still be inside a workable window before the next round of volatility hits."

## Lock-or-float guidance

For locks, the bias today is still defensive. If the borrower is inside 15 days, has a thin DTI, needs a specific payment target, or already likes the payment, locking makes sense. If the borrower is 21 to 30 days out and well-qualified, floating can still be reasonable, but only if you're setting the expectation that geopolitics and bond volatility can reverse an improvement fast. This is not a casual float market. It is a monitored float market.

## Key events and times

Today is light on major market-moving data, which means headlines can matter more than economics. The Conference Board Leading Economic Index is on the calendar for 10:00 a.m. Eastern. Treasury and rate watchers also get the Fed's H.15 selected interest rates release at 4:15 p.m. Eastern. Bigger picture, this week gets more important on Thursday with jobless claims at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, then Friday with S&P Global PMIs at 9:45 a.m. and new home sales at 10:00 a.m. The Fed's next FOMC meeting is July 28 to 29, and we're in the pre-meeting blackout window, so don't expect much fresh policy guidance from speakers.

## Market and housing context

Housing data still says affordability is the choke point. June housing starts bounced, but mostly because multifamily snapped back. Single-family starts were basically flat. At the same time, permits fell, which tells you builders are still cautious about future supply. Pending home sales dropped 5.4% in June, and builder confidence slipped to 34 in July, with buyer traffic at 23. Builders are still cutting prices and leaning on incentives. Translation: the market is not frozen, but demand is selective, payment-sensitive, and highly dependent on affordability.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When borrowers throw an online quote at you, do not fight the headline rate. Reframe the comparison. "That quote may be using more points, a shorter lock, a lower-risk profile, or assumptions that change once your file is underwritten. My job is to tell you what actually survives contact with the real loan." In this market, accuracy is a competitive advantage. A flashy teaser is easy. A reliable cash-to-close and payment plan is harder, and that's where you win.

## Pipeline play with refi angle

The pipeline play today is not just purchase follow-up. It is targeted refi triage. MBA data last week showed refi demand rising even with higher rates, led by FHA and VA refinances. That tells you some borrowers are moving for reasons other than chasing the perfect rate. Look for three buckets today: FHA or VA borrowers who still have a payment-improvement path, conventional borrowers carrying consumer debt who may benefit from a blended cash-flow conversation, and 2023 to 2024 buyers who need term, equity, or mortgage-insurance strategy rather than a dramatic rate drop.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Let's just wait for the Fed." Newer LOs say that when they want to sound informed, but it usually creates false hope and weakens urgency. Mortgage pricing does not wait politely for a Fed meeting. Bonds move early, often, and for reasons far beyond one policy decision.

## One advanced angle

The sharper angle for experienced LOs is to separate direction from spread. Even if Treasury yields improve, consumer mortgage rates do not always pass that through one-for-one. So your edge is not predicting a big rally. Your edge is helping the borrower act when the market gives a usable payment instead of waiting for a perfect headline that never fully reaches retail pricing.

## Borrower talk tracks

"If the house works and the payment works, we should treat a small rate improvement as a chance to execute, not a reason to get greedy."

"Online lenders are quoting a scenario. I'm advising you on an outcome."

"You don't need the perfect market. You need a payment and strategy you can live with."

"If rates improve later, we can talk about recapturing the opportunity. If the house is right now, the bigger risk may be losing the house, not missing an eighth."

## One concrete borrower example

If I'm talking to a first-time buyer who got spooked by a headline rate last week, I'd say: "Friday helped a little, and this morning hasn't taken that away in a major way. So instead of debating every market tick, let's pressure-test the payment at today's range. If the payment still works with taxes, insurance, and cash to close, then we can move forward confidently. If it doesn't, we solve that now with price point, seller credits, or product mix."

## One thing to watch

Watch oil and any Middle East headline more than you watch LO social media today. The cleanest threat to rates this week is energy-driven inflation fear feeding back into bond yields. If oil keeps climbing, mortgage pricing can stay sticky even without bad domestic data.

## Short coaching close for the team

Lead with clarity today. Borrowers do not need a market historian. They need someone who can translate noise into a decision. Your goal is simple: explain the payment, explain the risk, and make the next step feel easy to take.
