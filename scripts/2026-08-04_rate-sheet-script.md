# The Rate Sheet Script for Tuesday, August 4, 2026

Good morning team. Here is your Tuesday, August 4, 2026 Rate Sheet.

The market move is this: mortgage rates are starting the day more sideways than celebratory. Mortgage News Daily still has the 30-year fixed index at 6.82%, basically unchanged from Monday, while the 10-year Treasury is hovering around 4.70%. That tells you the bond market got some relief yesterday, but we are still operating in a high-rate range and the market has not given us a clean all-clear.

Why it matters is simple. This is the kind of market where one decent day in bonds can help the tone, but it does not automatically turn into materially better mortgage pricing. Rates are still being pushed around by inflation concerns, oil headlines, Treasury supply, and a Federal Reserve that may be done hiking for now but is not giving the market any comfort that cuts are close. So if you are talking to borrowers today, the message is not that rates got fixed overnight. The message is that the market is trying to stabilize after a rough move higher.

What this means in plain English: think of it like a fever that finally stopped rising. That is better, but the patient is not ready to run a marathon. For newer LOs, that means a flat rate morning can still be useful even if the note rate barely changes, because the bigger value is calmer expectations and better conversations. For experienced LOs, the sharper version is this: we may be shifting from panic pricing to range trading, and that changes how you frame urgency.

What to say to customers: "The market looks steadier this morning, but we are still in the higher range we moved into last week. So today is about making a smart financing decision, not waiting for a magic headline to rescue the payment."

Lock-or-float guidance: for closings inside 15 days, lock. For closings inside 30 days, the bias is still lock unless the borrower fully understands the risk around today’s data and this week’s jobs report. For 45 days and beyond, floating can still make sense if the borrower has flexibility and you are actively watching the market, but this is not the week to get casual. The practical advice is to protect anything near term and stay selective with floats.

Key events and times: today, Tuesday, August 4, trade data hits at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. JOLTS job openings and factory orders both hit at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. The Treasury refunding cycle also matters, with the 3-year note auction at 1:00 p.m. Eastern. Tomorrow, Wednesday, August 5, brings ADP at 8:15 a.m. Eastern and ISM services at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. Thursday has jobless claims and productivity at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Friday is still the big one with the July jobs report at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.

Market and housing context: the housing market is still giving buyers more leverage than the rate headlines alone would suggest. Realtor.com’s July report, published August 3, said the median list price was down 2.4% year over year, 20% of listings had a price cut, and pending sales were up from a year ago but the momentum is fading. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey last Thursday showed the average 30-year fixed at 6.66%, up from 6.58% the week before. MBA’s latest weekly survey showed mortgage applications fell 6.4%, with purchase demand still feeling the payment squeeze. That mix matters: payment pressure is real, but sellers are still having to compete.

Competitive context for online quote objections: when a borrower says, "I saw a lower rate online," slow the conversation down. "You probably saw a marketing rate, not your actual loan structure. The real comparison is what you can truly lock today with your credit profile, cash to close, timeline, and whether that quote depends on points or unrealistic assumptions." In this market, the professional advantage is translating the difference between an ad and an executable loan.

Pipeline play, with the required refi angle: today is a good database day for two buckets. First, preapproved buyers who drifted quiet after last week’s rate spike. Second, past clients with FHA, VA, or higher-coupon conventional loans who may not have a refinance opportunity today, but should be pre-positioned for one. The refi angle is not prediction. It is preparation. "Let’s refresh your numbers now so if the market gives us a better window later, we are ready to move fast instead of starting over."

One rookie mistake to avoid: do not tell borrowers that a flat day means rates are no longer a problem. That destroys trust the moment they compare yesterday’s payment to the one they hoped for. Your job is to explain that stability is helpful, but affordability is still the central issue.

One advanced angle: use the word "optionality" in a way the borrower can actually understand. The win is not always the lowest starting rate today. Sometimes the win is getting the house with seller help, preserving cash, structuring the payment intelligently, and leaving room to refinance later if spreads improve or the Treasury market calms down. Sophisticated advice sounds less like prediction and more like contingency planning.

Borrower talk tracks: "The market is calmer, but not cheap." "A steadier rate market helps us make better decisions even when the payment is still elevated." "You may have more negotiating power on the house today than borrowers had when rates were lower and competition was worse." "If the home works and the payment is responsible, we can protect the deal now and stay ready to improve the financing later."

One concrete borrower example: picture a first-time buyer who got discouraged last week when rates jumped and then saw a headline this morning that the market is steady. Your script can be: "Steady is good, but it does not mean the payment snaps back overnight. What it does mean is we can look at the real options in front of you today, including seller concessions, a smart lock strategy, and a refinance game plan if the market improves later." That keeps the borrower grounded while still moving the file forward.

One thing to watch: today’s 10:00 a.m. Eastern data block. If JOLTS or factory orders comes in stronger than expected, bonds can give back ground quickly and lenders can stay defensive. If the data shows a cooler labor or manufacturing picture, the market may lean into the stabilization story and give us a better tone heading into Friday.

Coaching close for the team: today is a day to sound calm, precise, and useful. Do not compete with headlines. Interpret them. If you can explain why a stable morning still matters, why seller leverage still matters, and why a purchase conversation can also create a future refinance relationship, you will sound more credible than the LO who just repeats the latest rate ad.
