# The Rate Sheet Script

August 10, 2026

Good morning. The market move coming into Monday is a little friendlier for rates than it looked late last week. Friday's weak jobs report pulled bond yields lower, Mortgage News Daily said top-tier 30-year fixed pricing improved to 6.74, and early Monday trading still has the market in a wait-and-see mode ahead of Wednesday's CPI report. So the headline is simple: the market got a little relief, but it has not earned the right to relax.

Why does that matter? Because mortgage pricing is still living in a tight range near the highest levels we have seen in about a year. The labor data helped, but it did not erase inflation risk, and it definitely did not erase the oil and geopolitical story that pushed long rates up in the first place. In other words, we got a breather, not a breakout.

What does that mean in plain English? It means the monthly payment conversation is still the main event. If you have a borrower who was barely comfortable at last week's payment, this move helps a little, but not enough to change the whole deal by itself. If you have a borrower who still has room in the budget, this market gives you a better chance to move them from hesitant to committed before the next inflation print resets the tone again.

What should we say to customers? Try this: "Rates improved a bit after the jobs report, but the bigger test is inflation this week. So the opportunity today is not that rates are suddenly low. The opportunity is that the market gave us a little breathing room before the next big number."

Lock-or-float guidance today is about time horizon and pain tolerance. If the borrower is closing in the next 15 days, lean lock. If they are inside 30 days and the deal is tight on DTI, cash to close, or seller cooperation, lean lock there too. If they are 45 days or more from closing and they have real payment cushion, floating into CPI can be reasonable, but frame it correctly: that is a calculated risk, not a free upside bet.

Here are the key events and times this week. Wednesday, August 12, the July CPI report hits at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Thursday, August 13, July PPI is out at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Friday, August 14, July retail sales lands at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Those are the numbers that can move bonds fast, and CPI is still the one that matters most for our lane.

The bigger market and housing context is mixed, and that matters for how we coach borrowers. Freddie Mac's weekly average 30-year fixed rate is 6.69, up for a fifth straight week. Redfin says pending sales just fell to a five-month low, but the same data set also says buyers have more time, more choices, and more negotiating power in many markets. That is an important combination: affordability is still stressed, but leverage is quietly getting better for prepared buyers.

That leads straight into the competitive context when borrowers throw out online quotes. Bankrate's national survey shows a 30-year fixed purchase APR at 6.82 and refinance APR at 6.96 on August 10, but the lead cards on those pages often assume points, ideal credit, specific LTVs, and clean owner-occupied scenarios. So when a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," the right response is not to argue. It is to translate. "Let's compare the full structure: rate, APR, points, lender fees, escrow assumptions, and how long you actually expect to keep this loan."

Pipeline play for today: use the market pause to create two lanes, not one. First, press the purchase pipeline with urgency around negotiation power. Buyers do not need perfect rates to win if they can get price cuts, seller credits, repairs, or a buydown. Second, and this is the required refi angle, scrub the database for cash-out and term-reduction households instead of waiting for a vanilla rate-and-term refi wave that still is not here. The borrowers worth calling are the ones consolidating high-interest debt, removing mortgage insurance, shortening term with stable income, or cleaning up a divorce or buyout situation where certainty matters more than chasing the absolute bottom rate.

One rookie mistake to avoid: leading with, "Rates are down." That is too vague, and in this market it can sound tone-deaf. Borrowers hear that and expect a dramatic payment change. Better language is, "The market improved modestly, which may help your payment, but the real value is how we structure the deal around today's market."

One advanced angle for experienced loan officers: the spread story still matters. HousingWire noted mortgage spreads around 2.01 are helping keep mortgage rates from fully matching the pain in Treasuries. That gives you a sharper customer explanation: "Mortgage rates are elevated, but they are not moving one-for-one with Treasury yields right now." Use that to sound informed without drowning the borrower in bond jargon.

Here are a few borrower talk tracks you can use today. "You do not need the perfect market to make a good housing decision." "If the home fits, the payment is safe, and we have a plan for the next 12 to 24 months, waiting for a headline rate can cost more than it saves." "The question is not just where rates are today. The question is whether today's deal gives you control, flexibility, and a payment you can live with."

One concrete borrower example. Say you have a first-time buyer looking at a $375,000 home with 10% down and solid but not oversized reserves. Last week they were focused on rate shock. Today, the better conversation is not "good news, rates fixed everything." It is, "We may have a little rate relief, and in this market we may also be able to negotiate seller help. If we can combine a modest pricing improvement with a closing-cost credit or temporary buydown, we can make your first 12 months much easier without waiting for the market to rescue us."

One thing to watch today is whether the bond market keeps respecting Friday's weaker jobs data or starts giving that rally back as traders reposition for CPI. If bond gains fade while oil firms back up, today's optimism can disappear quickly. So stay careful with same-day borrower promises.

Close the team with this: our edge today is not predicting the next headline. Our edge is translating a complicated market into calm advice. Newer LOs should focus on clarity and payment math. Veterans should press the leverage story, the spread story, and the structuring story. Either way, the assignment is the same: sound steady, sound useful, and make the borrower feel like there is a plan.
