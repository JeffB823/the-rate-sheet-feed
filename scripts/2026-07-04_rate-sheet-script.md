# The Rate Sheet Script for 2026-07-04

## Market move

Good morning team. Because today is Saturday, July 4, 2026, there is no live bond market move to react to. The real setup came from Thursday, July 2. Mortgage News Daily's daily index showed the average 30-year fixed at 6.60%, down from 6.65% the day before, while Freddie Mac's weekly survey showed the 30-year fixed averaging 6.43%, down from 6.49% the prior week. At the same time, the Fed's H.15 data showed the 10-year Treasury yield pushing up to 4.48% on July 1 from 4.44% on June 30.

## Why it matters

That combination matters because it tells you this is not a clean straight-line rally. Mortgage pricing improved a little, but the broader bond market still finished the week cautious. June payroll growth came in soft at 57,000, but unemployment held at a relatively low 4.2% and wages were still up 3.5% year over year. In plain terms, the labor market cooled, but not enough to make bond traders believe the Fed is suddenly on an easy path to cuts.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: rates got a little relief, not a true breakout lower. This is a market saying, "We may be slowing, but we are not convinced inflation risk is gone." That usually means better lock windows come and go fast.

## What to say to customers

Say it this way: "We did get a little improvement in mortgage pricing, but this is still a choppy market. I would treat this as a chance to make a smart decision, not as proof that rates are about to fall hard from here."

## Lock-or-float guidance

For closings inside 15 days, lock. For 15 to 30 days, I still lean lock unless the borrower has real payment cushion and understands that next week can reprice quickly. Float only when the file has room and the borrower has explicitly agreed to the risk. On a market like this, discipline beats optimism.

## Market and housing context

Housing is not frozen. It is just more selective. The MBA's latest weekly survey, released July 1, showed total applications basically flat week over week, purchase activity up 1%, and refis down 1%. Redfin's weekly housing update, published July 2 for the four weeks ending June 28, said the median U.S. housing payment posted its first year-over-year increase since October, while pending sales still edged up 0.4% week over week. And consumer mood is still fragile: the University of Michigan's final June sentiment index improved to 49.5 from May's 44.8, but that is still historically weak. So the opportunity is there, but borrowers need more explanation and more confidence from us.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I found a lower rate online," do not fight the ad. Reset the comparison. "The real question is not whether a website can show a lower headline. The real question is what rate, cost, monthly payment, and closing certainty you can actually lock on your file." In this market, execution is part of the value.

## Pipeline play

Today's pipeline play is two-sided. First, work purchase urgency because buyers have a little more negotiating room and slightly better financing than they had a week ago. Second, and this is required, run the refi angle even if you are not expecting a classic refi boom. Look for FHA borrowers who can shed monthly MI, conventional borrowers close to MI removal, debt-consolidation cash-out opportunities, and spring buyers who never got a true post-close payment strategy review. The refi call right now is about monthly cash flow, not chasing a trophy rate.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not say, "The jobs report was weak, so rates should keep falling." That is too simplistic and it trains borrowers to wait. The better version is: "Weak labor data can help rates, but mortgage pricing still depends on how bonds, inflation expectations, and the Fed outlook all react together."

## One advanced angle

Here is the veteran read: the payroll headline was soft, but the market still kept longer-term yields elevated. That usually means traders are more focused on sticky inflation risk and supply pressure than on one weaker growth print. If next week's FOMC minutes sound firm and Treasury auctions are weak, mortgage spreads can stay stubborn even without a hot data surprise.

## Borrower talk tracks

"You do not need a perfect rate to make a smart mortgage decision. You need a payment and a plan that still work if the market gets worse next week."

"This market is giving buyers more room to negotiate than the headlines suggest. A seller credit or price cut can matter just as much as a small rate move."

"If you already own, I am not calling to promise a miracle refinance. I am calling to see if we can improve your monthly cash flow, mortgage insurance, or debt structure."

## One concrete borrower example

Say you have a first-time buyer under contract at $410,000 who is hesitating because an online lender flashed a lower rate overnight. Your talk track is: "I am happy to compare it line by line, but I do not want you making a payment decision off a teaser. Right now we have slightly better market pricing, and we may also be able to use seller concessions to lower your real cost faster than waiting for a perfect headline rate."

## One thing to watch

Watch Wednesday, July 8 at 2:00 p.m. ET. If the FOMC minutes show a committee still worried about inflation and reluctant to guide toward cuts, the market may keep mortgage rates boxed in. If the tone sounds more concerned about growth, that could give bonds room to improve again.

## Coaching close

Your job this week is not to predict the next eighth in rate. Your job is to turn a noisy market into calm advice. Teach the borrower what changed, show them what still works, and make one purchase call and one refi call with a real payment strategy attached.
