# The Rate Sheet Script for Monday, August 3, 2026

Good morning team. Here is your Monday, August 3, 2026 Rate Sheet.

The market move is this: the bond market is trying to improve to start the week, but mortgage rates are still sitting near their recent highs. Mortgage News Daily showed the 30-year fixed index at 6.83% and the 10-year Treasury near 4.69% this morning, with Treasury yields lower as oil backed off and the market took in signs of de-escalation around Iran. That is the opening call today: bonds are getting a little relief, but mortgage pricing is still coming from a high base, so this is not a victory lap market.

Why it matters is simple. Mortgage rates do not need a Fed hike to stay elevated. They just need investors to keep worrying about inflation, Treasury supply, and whether the economy is still firm enough to keep long-term yields sticky. The Fed held rates steady on July 29, but the market is still treating policy as restrictive and still sees this week’s data, especially jobs, as a real test. In other words, today’s small improvement helps the tone, but it does not erase the broader pressure that pushed mortgage rates back toward their longer-term highs.

What this means in plain English: borrowers may hear that bonds are doing better today and assume rates are suddenly back down. That is not how this works. Think of it this way for newer LOs: the market is tapping the brakes, not turning the car around. For veterans, the sharper version is this: we have a risk-off rally in Treasuries, not a confirmed trend reversal in mortgage pricing.

What to say to customers: "The market looks a little better this morning, but rates are still being set off the higher range we moved into last week. So the conversation today is less about chasing the perfect headline and more about making a smart decision with the market we actually have."

Lock-or-float guidance: for closings inside 15 days, lock. For closings inside 30 days, the bias is still lock unless the borrower is unusually flexible and understands the risk around this week’s data. For 45 days and beyond, floating is still reasonable if you are watching the market closely, but do not sell today’s early Treasury rally as a durable turn until the week’s data confirms it. The practical rule is simple: protect near-term closings, stay nimble on longer timelines.

Key events and times: today, Monday, August 3, the S&P final manufacturing PMI is at 9:45 a.m. Eastern, ISM manufacturing is at 10:00 a.m. Eastern, and construction spending is also at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. Tomorrow, Tuesday, August 4, trade, factory orders, and JOLTS all hit in the morning. Wednesday brings ADP at 8:15 a.m. Eastern and ISM services at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. Thursday has jobless claims and productivity at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Friday is still the main event with the July jobs report at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.

Market and housing context: housing demand is still soft enough to create real negotiating room. Redfin said on July 30 that pending home sales fell 1.7% week over week to the lowest level since early April, and it said there are still hundreds of thousands more sellers than buyers nationally. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed at 6.66% for the week ending July 30, up from 6.58% a week earlier. MBA said mortgage applications fell 6.4% in its latest weekly survey, with purchase apps down 4% week over week, but still 3% above last year. That is an important mix to understand: affordability is still squeezing people, but inventory and negotiating leverage are better than they were when every deal was a knife fight.

Competitive context for online quote objections: when a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," your answer is not to argue first. Your answer is to translate. "A lot of online quotes are snapshots built around perfect-credit assumptions, points, or timing that changes before a real lock conversation happens. The real question is what you can actually lock today with your payment, your cash to close, and your goals." In a market like this, the gap between advertised pricing and executable pricing is one of our biggest opportunities to sound like adults.

Pipeline play, with the required refi angle: today is a strong call-block day for two groups. First, buyers who paused in the last 30 to 60 days because the payment got uncomfortable. Second, past clients with FHA, VA, temporary buydowns, or higher-rate second-lien debt. The refi angle is not, "Rates are about to drop." The refi angle is, "Let’s update your file now so when the market gives us a real opening, you are ready to move immediately instead of starting from zero." That keeps the message honest and keeps your database alive.

One rookie mistake to avoid: do not confuse a better bond morning with a better borrower outcome. If you tell someone rates are finally coming down because Treasuries rallied overnight, you are skipping the part where lenders still have to price from a much worse range than they had a few weeks ago.

One advanced angle: separate financing pain from housing leverage. A lot of LOs only talk about the note rate when rates are elevated. Better LOs also talk about seller concessions, price flexibility, buydown structure, and the value of having more inventory to choose from. In this market, the complete win is not always a lower rate today. Sometimes it is better terms on the house and a cleaner refinance runway later.

Borrower talk tracks: "The market is a little better this morning, but not enough to assume a full turnaround." "You do not win this market by chasing the lowest ad. You win it by structuring the right total deal." "Higher rates have slowed some buyers down, and that gives you more room to negotiate on the house." "If this home and payment work today, we can protect the deal now and stay ready to improve the financing later."

One concrete borrower example: picture a move-up buyer who paused two weeks ago because the monthly payment felt too tight and who woke up today seeing headlines about falling yields. Your script can be: "Today’s bond market is a little friendlier, which is good, but mortgage pricing is still near the higher end of the recent range. The bigger question is whether we can use today’s softer demand to negotiate seller help, control your cash to close, and put you in position to refinance if the market opens up later." That keeps the borrower grounded without sounding negative.

One thing to watch: today’s ISM manufacturing report at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. If the data shows firmer activity or hotter price pressure, the morning bond rally can fade fast. If it comes in softer, the market may start extending today’s improvement and give lenders a little more breathing room. Then Friday’s jobs report decides whether any rally has real legs.

Coaching close for the team: do not sell hope this week. Sell interpretation. Borrowers need someone who can explain why the market feels slightly better this morning and still say, with discipline, that the bigger rate story has not changed yet. Be the loan officer who can calm the room, frame the options, and tie every good purchase conversation to a future refinance plan without making predictions you cannot control.
