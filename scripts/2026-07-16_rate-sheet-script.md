# The Rate Sheet Script | July 16, 2026

Good morning team. Here is your Rate Sheet for Thursday, July 16th.

**Market move**

Mortgage pricing got a little breathing room yesterday. Mortgage News Daily's daily 30-year fixed index moved down to 6.64%, which was the lowest level in about a week after softer inflation data helped the bond market. That said, we are starting this morning with the 10-year Treasury back up near 4.57 and MBS a little softer before the open, so the tone today is better than earlier in the week, but not stable enough to get lazy.

**Why it matters**

The big story is not that rates suddenly got cheap. The story is that the market finally got a reason to stop pushing long-term yields higher. Tuesday's CPI cooled more than expected, Wednesday's PPI also came in softer, and that gave bond buyers enough confidence to pull mortgage rates off their recent highs. But this morning the market wants proof that the consumer is not re-accelerating, so retail sales, jobless claims, and later housing data all matter.

**What this means in plain English**

Here is the simple version you can use with a borrower: rates improved because inflation data looked a little friendlier, but we are not in an all-clear trend yet. Think of this as a step back from the ledge, not a full rate rally. If today's data runs hot, lenders can give some of that improvement right back.

**What to say to customers**

Try this: "We did get a little relief in the bond market, so today's pricing tone is better than it was a few days ago. But markets are still event-driven, and rates can move fast when new economic data hits. If the payment works and the house works, this is a market where certainty still has value."

For a shopper comparing you to an online quote, say: "An online quote is a snapshot. Your actual cost depends on timing, loan structure, credits, escrow strategy, and how the market moves between quote and lock. My job is to help you win the house and control the monthly payment, not just flash the lowest teaser number on one screen."

**Lock-or-float guidance**

For closings inside 15 days, I would lean lock unless the borrower has strong risk tolerance and meaningful room in the payment. We already got a decent bounce from the inflation data, and today's 8:30 releases can cut either way.

For 15 to 30 day files, this is more of a case-by-case call. If the borrower is happy with the payment and the debt-to-income ratio is tight, locking keeps you from turning a workable loan into a rescue project. If the file is strong and the borrower understands volatility, there is a reasonable argument for watching the market intraday, but that is not the same as assuming rates are headed straight down.

For 30-plus day files, a cautious float can make sense, but only with a real follow-up plan. Floating without a trigger, a communication cadence, or a lock threshold is not a strategy.

**Key events and times**

At 8:30 a.m. Eastern, the market gets retail sales, initial jobless claims, and the Philadelphia Fed survey. At 10:00 a.m., we get pending home sales and business inventories. At 12:00 p.m., Freddie Mac's weekly mortgage survey is due. Also on deck today are Fed speakers, including Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan at 12:30 p.m. Eastern and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson this evening. Tomorrow morning at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, housing starts and building permits hit, so some of today's rate tone can bleed into Friday.

**Market and housing context**

Housing still looks split. On one side, higher rates continue to cap affordability. On the other side, more supply is showing up and list prices are softening in parts of the country. Realtor.com said June asking prices were down 2.5% year over year, the sharpest drop in its data since 2017, while pending sales rose for a seventh straight month. That is a useful reminder: this is not a dead market. It is a more negotiable market.

Builder sentiment is also due at 10:00 a.m. today from NAHB, so by midday we should have a cleaner read on whether builders still feel boxed in by rate pressure or are seeing enough traffic to stabilize.

**Competitive context for online quote objections**

This is a strong market to reframe the conversation away from headline rate and toward execution. When rates are bouncing around with every inflation print and every Treasury move, shoppers do not just need a quote. They need a guide who can explain timing risk, seller-credit strategy, float-down possibilities if available, and how to structure the loan so the payment survives underwriting and appraisal reality.

A good line here is: "If two lenders are within an eighth to a quarter on rate, the real difference is usually whether someone is managing the transaction or just advertising into it."

**Pipeline play with the required refi angle**

Do not treat this mini-improvement as purchase-only news. Use it to reopen the top of the refinance funnel. Not because this is a massive refi wave. It is not. But because even modest rate improvement creates permission to call borrowers who passed six weeks ago.

The play is simple: pull cash-out candidates with revolving debt, recent equity growth, or upcoming payment stress. Then pull rate-term borrowers who are one meaningful step away from benefit, especially anyone who could improve payment through term mix, debt consolidation, or mortgage-insurance strategy rather than waiting for a perfect headline rate.

Your call can sound like this: "I am not calling because rates crashed. I am calling because the market improved enough that your numbers are worth re-checking, especially if we can combine rate movement with debt cleanup or payment restructuring."

**One rookie mistake to avoid**

Do not oversell one good inflation day as the start of a major downtrend. Newer LOs get in trouble when they promise improvement instead of explaining volatility. If you tell a borrower, "rates should be better tomorrow," you just turned market risk into a personal credibility problem.

**One advanced angle**

Experienced LOs should lean into spread language. Treasury improvement helps, but mortgage rates do not move one-for-one with the 10-year. In volatile periods, mortgage spreads stay wider because investors demand extra cushion. That means a borrower can hear "inflation cooled" on the news and still not see a dramatic rate drop. Explaining that gap cleanly makes you sound credible, not defensive.

**Borrower talk tracks**

"This market is a little better today, but it is still a live market, not a settled one."

"I would rather help you make a good decision with today's facts than gamble with tomorrow's headlines."

"If the payment works now, locking can protect progress. If the file has room and the borrower has patience, we can watch it with a plan, not with hope."

"More inventory and softer asking prices can matter just as much as a small rate move, because price and seller concessions also change the payment."

**One concrete borrower example**

Say you have a first-time buyer at $425,000 who was discouraged earlier this week because the payment felt stretched. Today is a good day to re-engage. Maybe the rate improvement alone does not transform the deal, but if the listing side is seeing more competition fatigue, you may be able to pair slightly better pricing with a seller credit or a small price concession. That is the coaching moment: do not sell the borrower on rates alone. Sell the full payment strategy.

**One thing to watch**

Watch how bonds react after the 8:30 a.m. data, not just the headline numbers. If retail sales come in firm and the 10-year keeps pushing higher from this morning's levels, lenders may claw back some of yesterday's gains. If the data is soft enough to keep recession fear low but disinflation hope alive, this improvement can hold together longer.

**Coaching close**

Lead with calm today. Borrowers do not need a market commentator. They need someone who can translate noise into a decision. Your edge is not predicting the next tick in rates. Your edge is helping people act clearly while everyone else is reacting emotionally.
