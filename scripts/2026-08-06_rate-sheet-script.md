# The Rate Sheet Script - August 6, 2026

Good morning. Here is your Rate Sheet for Thursday, August 6.

**Market move**

Mortgage rates came into today a little better than they were at the end of last week, but the bigger headline is that the improvement paused. Mortgage News Daily had the top tier 30-year fixed at 6.75% on Wednesday, unchanged from Tuesday after that earlier drop from 6.83% last Friday. So the move right now is not a fresh rally. It is more of a reset after a late-July backup.

**Why it matters**

That matters because when rates stop getting worse, buyers and refinance candidates start answering the phone again. But when rates are only modestly better, they still need help connecting the market move to their own payment, cash flow, or timing. This is not a day to promise a breakout lower. It is a day to explain that the market has backed away from the edge and is waiting for the next catalyst.

**What this means in plain English**

Plain English: the market gave borrowers a little breathing room, then hit pause. We are no longer talking about rates pressing to fresh highs, but we are also not in a clean rate-improvement story yet. Bonds have been trading off oil headlines, Fed expectations, and tomorrow’s jobs report risk, which means a borrower can wake up to a meaningfully different quote without anything changing in their personal file.

**What to say to customers**

Say this: "Rates improved from last week's highs, but the market is still jumpy. If you're buying, that small move can help, and if you're refinancing, we should look at whether the monthly savings or debt payoff math works today instead of waiting for a perfect number that may not show up."

**Lock-or-float guidance**

My guidance today is simple. If the borrower is inside 15 days, lock unless the deal has strong pricing cushion and the client clearly understands tomorrow's jobs-report risk. If they are 15 to 30 days out, floating can make sense only if they can tolerate volatility and you have already set expectations that one strong labor print can push rates back up fast. For shoppers, do not pitch float as a strategy. Pitch preparedness as a strategy.

**Key events and times**

This morning brought jobless claims and productivity data at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, Freddie Mac's weekly survey is due at 12:00 p.m. Eastern, and the real market event is Friday, August 7 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern with the July jobs report. That report is still the main tone-setter because bond traders keep translating labor strength into less urgency for the Fed to ease.

**Market and housing context**

The Fed held the fed funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75% on July 29, so the market is still trading the next question, not the last meeting. On the housing side, demand is soft but not collapsing. MBA said mortgage applications fell 2.9% in the latest weekly survey. Fannie Mae's weekly application data for the week ending July 31 also showed purchase volume down 2.5% week over week and refinance volume down 3.6%. Realtor.com said July list prices fell 2.4% year over year for a ninth straight monthly decline, while active listings rose 2.1% and price cuts hit 20% of listings. That tells you affordability is still tight, but sellers are getting more negotiable.

**Competitive context for online quote objections**

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," do not argue rate sheet to rate sheet. Say, "You probably did see a lower teaser, but the real question is what survives underwriting, points, LLPAs, escrows, and closing timeline. In this market, a quote is easy. A clean close with the right strategy is harder." Then bring it back to execution, not ego.

**Pipeline play with required refi angle**

Today’s pipeline play is a refi checkup, not a refi blast. Pull every closed loan from the last 12 to 18 months that landed in the low-7s or above, especially FHA and VA. The win does not have to be a dramatic rate drop. Look for borrowers who can lower payment, remove monthly debt pressure, shorten term without payment shock, or use a cash-out refinance to eliminate high-rate consumer debt. In a market like this, the refi opportunity is usually in the total monthly picture, not just the note rate headline.

**One rookie mistake to avoid**

Rookie mistake: telling borrowers rates are down and stopping there. Down from what matters. Down from yesterday means almost nothing to a consumer. Down from the highs they saw last week, and enough to change payment by real dollars, is the part they understand.

**One advanced angle**

Advanced angle: use softening home-price momentum as a financing advantage. More listings, more price cuts, and slower pending-sales growth give serious buyers leverage again. Veteran LOs should be pairing rate conversations with seller-paid buydown conversations, closing-cost credits, and contract structure. The financing edge right now is not just the rate. It is the combination of modestly better rates plus better seller behavior.

**Borrower talk tracks**

"You do not need the bottom in rates. You need a payment and plan that make sense."

"If the market gives us a better opening, we can take it. If it does not, I would rather have you approved, protected, and ready than chasing headlines."

"The internet can show you a number. My job is to show you what actually works for your file and your deadline."

**One concrete borrower example**

Here is a real way to frame it. On a $400,000 loan, moving from 7.125% to 6.75% cuts principal and interest by about $100 per month. That is not life-changing for every borrower, but for the right client it can be the difference between waiting and moving. And if that same borrower also gets a seller credit or pays off a couple of revolving balances, now the transaction starts to feel meaningfully different.

**One thing to watch**

Watch whether Friday's jobs report confirms a cooling labor market or slams the door on near-term rate optimism. If payrolls or unemployment come in hot for bonds, this week's calm can disappear quickly. If the data softens cleanly, the market has room to test lower again.

**Coaching close for the team**

Lead with interpretation today. Borrowers do not need a market historian. They need someone who can say, "Here is what changed, here is what it means for you, and here is the smart next move." If you do that well, you will sound credible to first-time buyers and sharp enough for the seasoned move-up client in the same conversation.
