# The Rate Sheet Script for Thursday, August 13, 2026

## Market move

Good morning. The market tone is a little better for mortgage pricing. After Wednesday's cooler-in-line CPI report, mortgage rates moved back down to three-week lows, and Mortgage News Daily's daily survey put the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.74%, down 5 basis points from the prior day. This morning, MBS are starting the day moderately stronger again, with the 10-year Treasury around 4.67%, so the early setup is friendly unless inflation data surprises.

## Why it matters

This matters because the market is telling us two things at once. First, inflation did not get worse in a way that would force the Fed's hand right now. Second, long-term rates are still elevated enough that borrowers are not suddenly getting a cheap-money window. In other words, this is relief, not a reset.

## What this means in plain English

For a consumer, today is not a story about miracle rates. It is a story about slightly better opportunity and slightly less pressure. The payment may not be dramatically different from last week, but the market stopped moving the wrong way for the moment, and that changes the conversation from panic to planning.

## What to say to customers

Here's the easy version:

"Rates improved a bit after inflation came in calmer than the market feared. We're not back in the low-rate world, but the pressure eased. If you're serious about buying, this is the kind of market where strategy matters more than chasing headlines."

For a more confident borrower:

"The bond market liked the inflation data, so pricing got a little better. The win here is not that rates are suddenly low. The win is that we have a cleaner window to make a smart decision without the market forcing your hand quite as much."

## Lock-or-float guidance

If the borrower is inside 15 days, has a tight DTI, or is payment-sensitive, locking still makes sense. This market can give back an improvement fast, especially with more inflation data and Treasury supply still in front of us.

If the borrower is 21 to 45 days out, structurally strong, and understands the risk, a cautious float can still be defended this morning, but only with clear guardrails. This is not a "set it and forget it" float. It is a "watch the data and be ready to act" float.

## Key events and times

Today, the biggest scheduled risk is July PPI at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Weekly jobless claims also hit at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Then the 30-year Treasury bond auction is on deck later today, and that can still move long-end yields and mortgage pricing.

Tomorrow, the next borrower-sentiment check is the preliminary University of Michigan consumer sentiment release at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. That one matters less for immediate rate sheets than CPI or PPI, but it can shape the inflation-expectations narrative.

## Market and housing context

The broader backdrop is still affordability strain. Freddie Mac's latest weekly survey was 6.69% for the 30-year fixed as of August 6, and Mortgage Bankers Association application data released August 12 showed total mortgage applications up 3.6% week over week, with both purchase and refi activity participating. That tells you demand is still alive whenever rates back off even a little.

At the same time, existing home sales for July slipped again this week, showing the market is still rate-capped. Buyers are interested, but many are only active when payments pencil out. That is the environment: demand is real, but affordability is the gatekeeper.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I saw a lower rate online," do not argue rate versus rate in a vacuum. Bring it back to structure.

"That online quote may be real, but the real question is what it costs to get there, how long it is locked, what assumptions are built in, and whether it still works once your actual file hits underwriting. My job is not to win a screenshot battle. My job is to keep your deal together at the payment and cash-to-close you can actually live with."

That is the right frame in this market. Consumers are shopping. Your edge is translating headline pricing into real execution.

## Pipeline play with refi angle

Today's pipeline play is simple: call your near-miss pre-approvals and your spring and early-summer shoppers who backed off when rates pushed higher. Not with "rates dropped, are you ready," but with "the market gave us a little room, so let's re-run the payment and see if the house or monthly number works now."

And the required refi angle: scrub the book for FHA, VA, and higher-coupon conventional borrowers who closed when the market was at the recent highs. This is not broad-stroke refi season, but it is absolutely the right day to identify who could benefit from a modest payment improvement, debt consolidation, or a future two-step strategy. Even if the full refi does not make sense today, planting the flag now matters.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not overpromise off one friendly inflation print. A newer LO's mistake is saying, "Looks like rates are coming down." That is too big a claim from too little evidence. Say, "The market improved a bit, and we're watching whether it holds." That keeps trust intact.

## One advanced angle

The advanced read is this: the market liked CPI, but the long end has not fully broken lower. That means bond investors are less worried about an immediate Fed move, but they are not fully relaxed about inflation, supply, or energy risk either. For seasoned LOs, that means you should sell optionality and execution, not just today's price. Your sophistication is in showing borrowers how to move before the market punishes hesitation.

## Borrower talk tracks

"You do not need the perfect rate to make a good decision. You need a payment, cash-to-close number, and timeline that fit your life."

"If you're waiting for the market to feel obvious, you usually end up buying after the opportunity already moved."

"The market gave us a little relief. Our job is to use it wisely, not assume it lasts forever."

## One concrete borrower example

Let's say you have a first-time buyer at $425,000 with 5% down who paused last week because the payment felt just out of reach. Today is a good day to call and say, "We picked up a little room from the bond market. It may not change your life, but it could be enough to reopen the conversation, especially if we pair it with a seller credit or a smart product adjustment." That is practical, honest, and actionable.

## One thing to watch

Watch whether PPI confirms the same message CPI sent yesterday. If producer inflation also behaves, the market can keep this better tone. If PPI runs hot, lenders can take back the improvement quickly.

## Coaching close for the team

Sell clarity today. Borrowers do not need a market historian. They need someone who can explain what changed, what did not change, and what smart next step to take right now.
