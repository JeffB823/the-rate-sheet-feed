# The Rate Sheet Script | August 26, 2026

Good morning team. The market gave us a small gift yesterday. Mortgage rates improved a bit after bonds rallied, and the main drivers were lower oil prices, softer consumer confidence, and weaker housing demand data. That does not mean rates are suddenly good again. It means the market got a little relief after being stretched, and that relief is keeping the door open for a better conversation with borrowers today.

Why that matters is simple. Mortgage rates do not need a perfect economy to improve. They just need the bond market to believe inflation pressure is cooling and growth is losing some momentum. That is what yesterday's data hinted at. The move was helpful, but not decisive, so today is less about victory laps and more about whether the bond market can hold the improvement through this morning's data and into Jackson Hole later this week.

In plain English, here is the takeaway: rates got a little better because the market saw less heat and more hesitation in the economy. Lower oil matters because energy feeds inflation fears. Weaker confidence matters because cautious consumers usually spend less aggressively. Soft housing data matters because it tells investors higher rates are still doing their job and slowing demand. When all three line up, rates can catch a break.

What to say to customers is this: "We did get a little improvement in the market, but we are not in a straight line lower. The bond market is trading headline to headline right now, so today's quote is better described as an opportunity window than a new trend."

Lock-or-float guidance for today: if the borrower is inside 21 days, I would lean lock unless they have strong risk tolerance and a very clean file. If they are 30 to 45 days out, floating can still make sense, but only with the expectation that Friday's inflation data and Fed messaging could swing pricing quickly. The practical advice is to treat float like a monitored strategy, not a hope strategy.

Key events and times today: durable goods orders at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, the second estimate of Q2 GDP at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, crude inventories at 10:30 a.m. Eastern, and Treasury auctions in the afternoon that can still shape bond tone. Then look ahead to Thursday's 7-year note auction and Friday's key events: PCE inflation at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, final Michigan sentiment at 10:00 a.m. Eastern, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote at 10:00 a.m. Eastern on Friday, August 28.

The broader market and housing context is getting easier to explain. Consumer confidence slipped in August because expectations weakened, not because current conditions collapsed. New home sales fell hard in the July report, and both FHFA and Case-Shiller showed home prices still rising nominally but with less real strength once you account for inflation. That is a useful message for buyers: home prices are not crashing nationally, but affordability pressure is clearly cooling demand and slowing momentum.

The competitive context matters because online quote shoppers are still going to say, "I found something lower." Do not fight that with a rate-sheet monologue. Say this instead: "A headline quote is easy to make look better when you do not line up the lock period, points, escrows, and how realistic the approval is. My job is to show you the real payment, the cash needed, and the probability of closing on time." In this market, speed, certainty, and clarity still beat a mystery quote.

Pipeline play for today, and this one needs a refi angle: re-open every borrower from the past 60 to 120 days who passed because the payment felt too high, and every recent funded client who took a higher note rate to win the house. The message is not "rates are low again." The message is, "The market just gave us a little improvement, and I want to check whether your payment, cash-to-close, or long-term strategy looks any better today than it did when you last looked." On the refi side, focus on cash-out debt consolidation, recent buyers who may want to drop MI sooner with updated value, and borrowers sitting on adjustable debt who need payment stability more than they need a perfect rate.

One rookie mistake to avoid: telling borrowers the Fed controls mortgage rates directly. It creates confusion and sets you up to sound wrong when the Fed holds steady and mortgage pricing still moves. The cleaner explanation is that mortgage rates follow the bond market, inflation expectations, and investor risk appetite much more closely than the overnight Fed funds rate.

One advanced angle for experienced originators: yesterday's move was not just about one data point. It was a duration story tied to softer growth signals, lower energy pressure, and a market still testing how much Treasury support and upcoming Fed communication can calm long-end yields. That gives you better language with financial planners, builders, and higher-information clients. You can say, "The market is trying to decide whether last week's rate spike was an overshoot or the start of a higher range. Yesterday favored overshoot, but this week's inflation and Fed messaging still have veto power."

Borrower talk tracks for today:

"If you want certainty, this is a reasonable day to lock. If you want to float, we need a real trigger for when we stop waiting."

"The market improved a little, but not enough for me to call it a trend. I would rather be honest about that than overpromise."

"If another lender is quoting better, let's compare the actual structure side by side and find out whether it is truly better or just presented better."

"You do not need the perfect rate to make a smart decision. You need a payment and plan that still make sense if the market gets choppy again."

One concrete borrower example: think about a first-time buyer who paused last week because the payment on a $425,000 purchase felt just out of reach. Today, you call and say, "The market improved a bit since we last talked. It may not be dramatic, but sometimes a small rate move plus a seller credit tweak or a different MI structure is enough to get the payment back into range. Want me to rerun it before you walk away from the house?" That is the kind of call that creates trust because it is specific, low-pressure, and useful.

One thing to watch today is whether bonds hold gains after the 8:30 data. If stronger-than-expected GDP or durable goods pushes yields back up, yesterday's improvement can fade fast. If the data reinforces the slowdown story, lenders may be able to preserve or even extend the better tone into the day.

Coaching close for the team: do not sell the rate. Sell the interpretation. Customers can find numbers anywhere. What they remember is the loan officer who made the market make sense and helped them act before the window moved.
