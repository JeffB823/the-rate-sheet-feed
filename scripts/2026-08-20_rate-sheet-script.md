# The Rate Sheet | Thursday, August 20, 2026

## Market Move

Good morning team. Today starts with a slightly better headline than the market felt two days ago, but not with a clean all-clear. Mortgage News Daily's Wednesday, August 19 read put the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.72%, down from 6.75% the day before, after Treasury's buyback announcement helped long bonds recover. But this morning's live setup is more cautious than celebratory. Mortgage-backed securities are a touch weaker early, and Mortgage News Daily's rate trend says that likely means minimal impact on mortgage rates today, not a real improvement.

## Why It Matters

This matters because the market is trying to decide whether yesterday's bond bounce was the start of relief or just a pause in a still-stressed rate environment. The bond market got help when Treasury said it would increase long-end buybacks, which pushed long-term yields lower on Wednesday. But the Fed minutes released Wednesday, August 19 also reminded the market that inflation risk is still very alive. Several policymakers were ready to hike, and many said higher rates may still be needed if inflation does not cool further. That keeps mortgage pricing from relaxing too quickly.

## What This Means in Plain English

Plain English: rates got a little less bad, but they have not gotten meaningfully good. Think of it like a borrower who stopped sliding backward but is not moving forward yet. The market found some footing, but it still does not trust that inflation and long-term borrowing costs are really under control.

## What to Say to Customers

Try this: "The market improved a little yesterday, but not enough to call this a real rate break yet. We are in a better spot than the start of the week, but this is still a market where small setbacks can hit pricing fast."

Sharper version: "Mortgage rates follow the bond market, but they do not mirror every intraday move one-for-one. Right now bonds are trying to stabilize, while lenders are still protecting themselves against volatility."

## Lock-or-Float Guidance

For closings inside 15 days, this is still a lock-first market. You have not earned enough improvement to justify gambling with a near-term payment.

For 15 to 30 days, I would call it a cautious float only if the borrower understands the risk and you are watching the screen closely after the morning data. This is a tactical float, not a hopeful float.

For 30-plus days, floating is still reasonable, but frame it correctly. You are floating because there is time to manage volatility, not because the market has clearly turned.

## Market and Housing Context

The housing backdrop still says affordability is the brake pedal. Census reported on Tuesday, August 18 that July housing starts fell 12.4% from June, and single-family starts fell 9.9%, even while permits rose 5.0%. That is a useful split for your calls: builders still want to build, but they are not blindly pushing product into a payment-sensitive market.

Existing-home sales data released by NAR on Tuesday, August 11 also showed the same story. July sales fell 1.7% month over month, inventory was 1.54 million homes, and supply held at 4.6 months. Meanwhile, Realtor.com's latest weekly housing read through August 8 showed price reductions topping 100,000 for the fifth straight week. Translation: homes are still moving, but the market is forcing sellers to negotiate more often.

On the borrower mood side, the University of Michigan's preliminary August sentiment reading fell to 51 from 55.2 in July, and the survey said expected business conditions dropped 11% in the short run and 17% in the long run. That matters for us because uncertain borrowers do not just shop rates harder. They delay decisions longer.

## Competitive Context for Online Quote Objections

When a borrower says, "I found a lower rate online," do not argue with the screenshot. Slow the conversation down.

Say: "That may be a real quote, but a real quote is not the same as a real outcome. Let's line up the rate, points, lender fees, lock period, mortgage insurance, and cash to close before we decide it is actually better."

Then add: "In this market, a quote can look better simply because it is carrying more assumptions than your actual file can support."

## Pipeline Play

Today's pipeline play has to include the refi angle. The MBA's weekly survey released Wednesday, August 19 showed total applications down 0.4% week over week, purchase activity down 2%, and refis taking a 41.9% share of activity. That is the signal: purchase borrowers are still hesitating, but the second the market cracks open even a little, refinance attention comes back fast.

So split your outreach in two blocks.

First, call your active shoppers who have gone quiet. Your message is not "rates are fixed." Your message is "the market stopped getting worse this week, and that gives us a reason to review payment strategy again."

Second, work the refi bench intentionally. Call FHA, VA, and higher-coupon conventional borrowers with heavy consumer debt, pending life changes, or monthly cash-flow stress. You are not pitching a same-day refinance. You are building a ready list so that if the next quarter gives us a better window, your borrower is already documented, educated, and emotionally engaged.

## One Rookie Mistake to Avoid

Do not tell borrowers that lower Treasury yields automatically mean better mortgage pricing today. Treasury improvement helps, but mortgage spreads and lender margins can delay or dilute that benefit.

## One Advanced Angle

The advanced angle today is lender lag. Veteran LOs know the market can improve before retail pricing fully catches up. That is a great advisor conversation with sophisticated borrowers: "The bond market may heal first, and the consumer rate sheet may catch up later, so we watch both the market move and lender behavior before we call the window real."

## Borrower Talk Tracks

"The market is calmer than it was earlier this week, but not calm enough yet to assume waiting will pay off."

"My job is not to guess the perfect day. My job is to help you act when the math works and protect you when the market is unstable."

"If another lender's quote is truly better after we match the details, I will tell you. But we need to compare the whole loan, not just the headline rate."

## One Concrete Borrower Example

Think about a move-up buyer who is frozen because the payment feels high and every online quote makes the market look random. The conversation is: "You do not need the perfect rate headline to make a good decision. You need a payment strategy that works today, negotiating leverage with the seller, and a clear refinance plan if the market gives us a better window later. We can build that without pretending this market is easy."

## One Thing to Watch

Watch whether today's economic data keeps the market in stabilization mode or pushes traders back into inflation fear. If claims stay low and business activity stays firm, the market may read that as one more reason the Fed cannot relax yet.

## Coaching Close

Today's edge is calm translation. Newer LOs should remember that borrowers hear "rates moved" and think "my payment changed a lot." Veteran LOs should use that moment to separate market noise from borrower math. If you can explain the difference clearly, you win trust before you win the loan.
