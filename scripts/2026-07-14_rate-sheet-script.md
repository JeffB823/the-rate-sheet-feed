# The Rate Sheet Script

July 14, 2026

Good morning team. Here’s your rate-sheet read for Tuesday, July 14th.

## Market Move

The headline move is that mortgage rates lost ground yesterday, and they did it fast enough that borrowers will feel it. Mortgage News Daily moved its daily 30-year fixed average up to 6.75% on July 13, which was the highest read in nearly a month. The bond market also stayed under pressure, with the 10-year Treasury trading around 4.62% early this morning, so we are starting the day from a weaker level and heading straight into CPI at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.

## Why It Matters

This is not just a random rate bump. The market spent the last 24 hours repricing inflation risk and trimming back easy-Fed hopes. Oil moved higher, Treasury yields pushed up, and Fed commentary stayed firm enough that traders are less comfortable assuming near-term relief. When that happens, mortgage-backed securities usually cheapen, lenders get more defensive, and consumer pricing worsens even before a borrower sees the official rate sheet.

## What This Means In Plain English

Here’s the simple version: money got more expensive yesterday, so home financing got a little worse. For a borrower, that usually means one of three things: a higher rate, a higher cost to keep the same rate, or less room to solve a payment problem with seller credit and structure alone. The main lesson for newer LOs is that rates do not move because lenders woke up in a bad mood. They move because the bond market decides inflation and Fed risk deserve a bigger cushion.

## What To Say To Customers

Try this: "The market got a little worse yesterday because bond investors are worried inflation may stay sticky. That doesn’t mean your deal is broken, but it does mean today’s best move is to structure with intent instead of assuming we’ll get a free improvement later."

For experienced reps, sharpen it like this: "Yesterday was a bond-led repricing, not branch noise. If inflation data stays hot, lenders can stay defensive longer than borrowers expect."

## Lock-Or-Float Guidance

For closings inside 15 days, the default posture is lock. For 15 to 30 days, lean lock unless the borrower can tolerate volatility and you have a real reason to wait. For longer windows, floating is only defensible if the client understands that today’s CPI can either reopen the rally or push the market into another round of reprices. In plain English: if the payment already works, protect it. Do not float just because the borrower hopes tomorrow feels better than today.

## Key Events And Times

Today’s big event is CPI at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday, July 14. Also on deck today is Fed Governor Christopher Waller at 9:15 a.m. and Fed Governor Kevin Warsh at 10:00 a.m. Tomorrow, July 15, brings PPI at 8:30 a.m. and the Beige Book at 2:00 p.m. Thursday, July 16, is Retail Sales at 8:30 a.m. Friday, July 17, gives us Housing Starts at 8:30 a.m. and preliminary consumer sentiment at 10:00 a.m. This week is front-loaded with market-moving data, so expect lender pricing to stay jumpy.

## Market And Housing Context

Freddie Mac’s last weekly survey still had the average 30-year fixed below where daily market pricing sits now, which matters because many consumers are anchored to old headlines and not same-day execution. At the same time, the New York Fed’s June Survey of Consumer Expectations showed households still expect home prices to keep rising over the next year. That mix matters: buyers still believe housing will be more expensive later, but they are payment-sensitive right now. That is exactly the kind of market where trusted advice beats rate-chasing.

## Competitive Context For Online Quote Objections

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," the right response is not to argue about one screenshot. It is to slow the conversation down and translate the quote. Say: "Online quotes are often built around perfect-credit, perfect-equity, perfect-timing assumptions. The real comparison is total cash, total payment, and whether that quote survives underwriting." In a volatile bond market, competitor quotes also get stale fast. Your edge is not winning the first number. Your edge is proving which number is real.

## Pipeline Play With Refi Angle

Today’s pipeline play is a refinance triage pass, not a blind rate blast. Pull the near-miss files, cash-out conversations, and any borrower who passed because the monthly savings was too thin last time. The script is: "I’m not calling because rates magically improved. I’m calling because payment strategy changes every time the market moves, and I want to see whether term mix, debt payoff, or timing changes the math." Refi volume returns in layers. The first layer is not the obvious rate-and-term home run. It is the borrower whose financial plan improves even before the headline rate does.

## One Rookie Mistake To Avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Let’s wait and see what the Fed does," as if the Fed announcement is the only thing that moves mortgage rates. Mortgage pricing often moves before the Fed, around the Fed, and sometimes against the Fed because bonds trade the data, the outlook, and the inflation path. That line makes you sound reactive instead of informed.

## One Advanced Angle

Here’s the higher-level read: if CPI comes in soft, the market can rally quickly because positioning turned more defensive yesterday. But if CPI is firm, the damage can extend because traders just started rebuilding inflation premium. Translation for veterans: today has asymmetric repricing risk, and fallout conversations should be prepared before the release, not after.

## Borrower Talk Tracks

"My job isn’t to guess the perfect day. My job is to help you make a decision that still feels smart if the market gets worse tomorrow."

"A low quote is only useful if it’s still there when we lock and if the fees match the story."

"If the home and payment both fit, protecting the plan usually matters more than squeezing for one last eighth."

## One Concrete Borrower Example

Think about a first-time buyer shopping at the top of approval. Yesterday’s move may not kill the deal, but it can reduce room for appraisal gap cash, temporary buydown strategy, or seller-credit flexibility. The right call is not, "Bad news, rates are up." The right call is, "We still have options, but today is a day to tighten the structure early so the market does not choose for us."

## One Thing To Watch

Watch whether lenders improve or worsen aggressively after CPI rather than only watching the headline number itself. If inflation comes in cooler and rate sheets barely improve, that tells you lenders still do not trust the rally. If inflation runs hot and reprices come quickly, that confirms the market is in protect-margin mode and lock conversations need to get firmer.

## Coaching Close For The Team

Today is a translator day. Borrowers do not need a macro lecture. They need someone who can turn bond-market stress into a calm, useful recommendation. Be the LO who explains the payment, the risk, and the next move clearly enough that the customer stops shopping for noise.
