# The Rate Sheet Script

Tuesday, July 21, 2026

## Market move

Good morning. The headline today is simple: mortgage pricing is still under pressure. Monday pushed rates higher again, with Mortgage News Daily's daily 30-year fixed index moving up to 6.71%, and the bond market weakness that caused that move was tied mostly to higher oil-driven inflation fears and another rough day for mortgage-backed securities. This morning feels more like damage control than a true recovery, so the tone is still defensive.

## Why it matters

What matters is not just that Treasury yields moved up yesterday. It is that mortgage-backed securities took the hit too, and that is what lenders actually price from. When oil rises and traders worry inflation could hang around longer, bond prices usually fall, yields rise, and mortgage rate sheets get worse before the average buyer has even finished breakfast.

## What this means in plain English

In plain English, buyers did not suddenly lose the ability to qualify overnight, but the market just made monthly payments a little less forgiving. A small rate move does not sound dramatic on paper, but to a payment-sensitive borrower it can be the difference between feeling comfortable and feeling squeezed.

## What to say to customers

Here is the customer version: "The market pushed mortgage rates a bit higher again because bond traders got nervous about inflation, especially with energy back in the conversation. Nothing is broken in your file. This is just one of those weeks where waiting has not been paying people yet."

## Lock-or-float guidance

For loans closing soon, the bias is still toward locking. If a borrower is inside 21 days, or if the payment already feels tight, protecting the deal makes more sense than gambling on a headline-driven rally. If the borrower has more time and strong pricing tolerance, floating is still possible, but it should be framed as a managed risk decision, not a hope strategy. Right now this is a market that has been quick to punish optimism and slow to reward it.

## Key events and times

Today, Tuesday, July 21, is light on major scheduled U.S. economic data, so the bond market can still move on oil, headlines, and general risk tone. The next bigger scheduled items are Thursday's jobless claims at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, Friday's S&P Global PMIs at 9:45 a.m. Eastern, and Friday's new home sales report at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. The next FOMC meeting is July 28 through July 29, so the market is already starting to think ahead to next week.

## Market and housing context

Housing still looks rate-sensitive, not broken. NAR says June existing-home sales fell 2.4% month over month, and pending home sales also slipped across all four regions, which is another reminder that affordability is still the gatekeeper. At the same time, June housing starts jumped overall, but single-family starts were basically flat, so builders are active while the resale market still feels the drag from payment shock.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I'm seeing something lower online," do not argue with the screenshot. Slow it down and say, "You may be. The real question is whether that quote includes the same points, the same lock period, the same mortgage insurance structure, and the same cash-to-close." In this market, headline rates are cheap to advertise and expensive to actually deliver. Your edge is clarity, not defensiveness.

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

Today's pipeline play is the refi conversation that is hiding inside the purchase conversation. If a borrower is stretching a little to buy now, give them permission to separate the home decision from the permanent financing decision. Say, "We can make the purchase decision based on the home you want now, and if the rate market improves later, we can review whether a no-cash-out refi cleans this up." That keeps the purchase alive without pretending today's rate is ideal.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Rates should come down soon." That is not advice. That is a guess, and this market has punished that guess repeatedly. A better sentence is, "Rates can improve, but today's market is still reacting quickly to inflation and geopolitical headlines, so I don't want to build your plan on hope."

## One advanced angle

Here is the sharper read for experienced LOs: watch the spread story, not just the 10-year. This morning is a good example of why borrowers get confused. Treasury yields can look a touch better while MBS still feel soft, which means consumer mortgage pricing may not improve as much as a casual market watcher expects. That is a strong way to explain why a flashy market headline does not always translate into a better quote.

## Borrower talk tracks

Try this: "If you love the house and the payment works today, we should protect the opportunity. If the market gives us a better refinance window later, great. If it doesn't, you still won the house." For a shopper who is hesitating, try this: "The bigger risk right now has not been missing a huge rate drop. It has been losing buying power while waiting for one."

## One concrete borrower example

Say you have a first-time buyer who is already near the top of their comfort range and wants to close in three weeks. Today's guidance is straightforward: lock the loan, show the exact payment, and take the future-rate conversation out of today's approval decision. Then plant the follow-up seed by telling them you will watch for refinance opportunities after closing instead of asking them to absorb market risk right now.

## One thing to watch

Watch whether energy-driven inflation fear keeps controlling the bond trade. If oil pressure eases and MBS finally catch up, we could get a better tone later this week. If not, lenders may stay cautious even on quieter data days.

## Coaching close for the team

Your job today is to sound calmer than the market. Do not sell rate predictions. Sell explanation, structure, and next steps. Borrowers do not need a trading desk. They need someone who can help them make a good decision in a noisy week.
