# The Rate Sheet Script | August 8, 2026

**Market move**

Here is the headline for Saturday, August 8. The bond market got a relief rally on Friday after a softer jobs report, and mortgage pricing followed with a small improvement. Mortgage News Daily had the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.74% on August 7, down from 6.77%, and called it the lowest reading since July 20. So the move is better, but not dramatically better. This is a bounce, not a breakout.

**Why it matters**

Why does that matter? Because the same report that helped bonds also changed the Fed conversation. The July jobs report showed payrolls down 23,000, unemployment at 4.1%, and another 103,000 jobs revised out of May and June. That pushed the 10-year Treasury down into the mid-4.6s by Friday afternoon and cooled September hike odds. In plain terms, the market heard one message: growth may be soft enough to slow the Fed down, at least for now.

**What this means in plain English**

If you are explaining this to a borrower, keep it simple. Rates did not fall because lenders suddenly got generous. Rates improved because investors got a little less worried about the economy overheating and a little less convinced the Fed has to tighten again right away. That helps affordability on the margin, but it does not erase the payment shock borrowers have been feeling.

**What to say to customers**

The line to borrowers is this: "We finally got a little breathing room, but not a full reset. If you liked the home before Friday, you should still like the home now. The win is that the market gave us a slightly better window to structure the payment." That keeps the conversation grounded in monthly payment, not headlines.

**Lock-or-float guidance**

For lock guidance, stay disciplined. If the borrower is inside 30 days, or if the deal is payment-sensitive and already works, locking is still the cleaner call. Next week has enough inflation risk to give back Friday’s improvement fast. Floating makes more sense when the closing is farther out, the borrower has cushion, and you are consciously betting that softer growth matters more than sticky inflation. This is still a defensive lock market, just a slightly friendlier one than it was 48 hours ago.

**Key events and times**

The next big checkpoints are all next week. Wednesday, August 12 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern is CPI. Thursday, August 13 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern is PPI. Friday, August 14 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern is retail sales, and Friday at 10:00 a.m. Eastern is the preliminary August University of Michigan consumer sentiment read. If Friday’s rate improvement is real, it has to survive that sequence.

**Market and housing context**

The housing backdrop is still giving good loan officers something to work with. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey moved the 30-year fixed up to 6.69% as of August 6, so broad mortgage-rate averages still look elevated even after Friday’s daily dip. On housing demand, Redfin said pending sales fell to a five-month low and buyers have more time, more choices, and more negotiating power. That matters because in this market, the financing conversation is no longer just "Can they qualify?" It is also "Can they negotiate terms, credits, and timing well enough to offset today’s rate environment?"

**Competitive context for online quote objections**

When a borrower says, "The online lender is lower," do not argue rate versus rate in a vacuum. Slow the conversation down. Ask whether that quote is locked, whether it includes points, what credit score and down payment assumptions were used, whether escrows are waived, and how long the lock lasts. A lot of online quotes are marketing numbers, not execution numbers. The sharp response is: "If their quote is real, we can compare it line by line. If it is just a headline rate with extra cost buried in points or assumptions, that is not actually the cheaper loan."

**Pipeline play with required refi angle**

Today’s pipeline play is a two-lane follow-up. First, re-open purchase leads that went cold when rates spiked late in July. Tell them the market finally gave us a little room and it is worth re-running the payment with seller credits in mind. Second, work the refi angle the right way. This is not a mass rate-and-term refi story yet. It is a targeted refi story for FHA-to-conventional MI removal, VA IRRRL prospects, cash-out borrowers consolidating higher-rate consumer debt, and past clients who can improve payment strategy even if the note rate does not drop dramatically. Refi talk should be tactical, not wishful.

**One rookie mistake to avoid**

The rookie mistake today is overselling the rally. If you tell borrowers "rates are dropping," and CPI reverses the market next week, you lose credibility. Say "we got some improvement" instead. Precision builds trust.

**One advanced angle**

The advanced angle is this: the market is starting to separate growth fear from inflation fear. Friday’s jobs report helped the growth side, but next week’s inflation data can still dominate pricing. Veteran loan officers should use that nuance on calls. The best phrasing is: "The bond market wants to believe the economy is cooling, but it still needs proof that inflation is cooling too." That is sophisticated without sounding academic.

**Borrower talk tracks**

Use talk tracks like these. "You do not need the perfect rate to make a smart move. You need a payment and plan that still make sense if the market stays bumpy." Or: "This market rewards prepared buyers, because sellers are more negotiable when demand slows." And for skeptical shoppers: "If the house and payment fit now, we can always improve the financing later if the market gives us that chance."

**One concrete borrower example**

Here is a clean example. Say you have a first-time buyer who paused two weeks ago because the payment on a $425,000 purchase felt stretched. Your move now is not to lead with "good news, rates fell." Your move is to say, "The market improved a bit, and more importantly, buyers have better leverage again. Let’s see whether a small rate improvement plus a seller credit changes the monthly payment enough to get you comfortable." That reframes the win as structure, not hope.

**One thing to watch**

One thing to watch is whether next week’s CPI confirms or rejects Friday’s bond rally. If inflation runs hot, the market will treat the jobs miss as noise and push rates back up. If inflation eases, then Friday may look like the first real opening after a rough late-July run.

**Coaching close for the team**

The coaching close is simple. Do not sell relief as victory. Sell clarity. Borrowers do not need us to predict every rate move. They need us to explain what changed, what it means for their payment, and what the next smart step is. That is how you sound steady in a market that still is not.
