# The Rate Sheet Script for Wednesday, July 29, 2026

Good morning team. As of early Wednesday, July 29, 2026, the market tone is a little worse again after yesterday's brief bond-market relief. Mortgage News Daily still has the average 30-year fixed at 6.76%, which is near the upper end of this year's range, and early market pricing shows MBS a touch weaker with the 10-year Treasury back around 4.61%. In plain English, yesterday helped calm nerves, but it did not reset the game. We are still one headline away from volatility, and today is a headline-heavy day.

Why it matters is simple. Mortgage rates do not need a full-blown Fed hike to stay elevated. If oil jumps, inflation fears stay alive, Treasury yields stay sticky, and mortgage pricing stays defensive. That is what we are dealing with this morning. The market got a little hope on Tuesday from calmer war headlines, then gave some of that back as traders refocused on oil and the Fed. So the message for borrowers is not "rates are crashing." The message is "the market tried to improve, but conviction is still weak."

What this means in plain English: we are in a market where the floor under rates is still too high for buyers to feel comfortable, but the ceiling is also not guaranteed to blow out unless inflation fear gets worse. That creates a choppy environment, not a clean trend. Newer LOs should hear this clearly: choppy markets reward calm language, fast follow-up, and simple advice. Veterans should hear the sharper version: sell certainty when the market is unstable, and sell optionality when the borrower has time.

What to say to customers: "The market improved a little yesterday, but this morning we're giving some of that back. So this is still a higher-rate market, not a lower-rate market pretending to come back. If you're buying soon, the smart move is to build around payment comfort and a plan, not wait for a perfect headline."

Lock-or-float guidance: if the borrower is closing in the next 15 to 30 days, lean lock unless there is a very strong reason not to. We have a Fed decision today at 2:00 p.m. Eastern and a Fed press conference at 2:30 p.m. Eastern, followed by GDP and PCE tomorrow, Thursday, July 30, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. That is too much event risk to float casually. If the borrower is 45 days or more from closing and has strong tolerance for movement, you can frame float as a managed risk, but only with clear guardrails and fast re-lock communication.

Key events and times: today the Federal Reserve releases its statement at 2:00 p.m. Eastern, then Chair Warsh speaks at 2:30 p.m. Eastern. Tomorrow at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, we get advance second-quarter GDP and June Personal Income and Outlays, which includes PCE inflation. Those two windows matter more than almost anything else on the screen right now because they can change the market's story about inflation, growth, and the next Fed move.

Market and housing context: yesterday's borrower-sentiment data was softer. The Conference Board said consumer confidence fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June, and the present-situation view weakened again. Housing data also said affordability is still the choke point, not demand enthusiasm. FHFA reported prices rose 0.3% in May and 2.2% year over year, while Census said the homeownership rate held at 65.0% in the second quarter and homeowner vacancy stayed just 1.2%. That is a useful combo: buyers are more cautious, but supply is still not loose enough to create broad-based price relief.

Competitive context for online quote objections: this morning, public quote sites are still all over the place. Bankrate showed a 30-year fixed average around 6.75% with a 6.82% APR, while NerdWallet's national 30-year fixed average was closer to 6.54% to 6.55%. That spread is the reminder. Online rates are marketing snapshots, not your borrower's actual execution. So when a customer says, "I saw a lower rate online," the answer is: "You probably did. The real question is whether that quote matches your credit, equity, occupancy, lock timing, points, and fees. I can show you the apples-to-apples version in five minutes."

Pipeline play, with the required refi angle: do not run a generic "rates dropped, who wants to refi?" campaign, because that is not the market we are in. Instead, run a payment-planning refi play. Call past customers who took higher-cost temporary structures, cash-out debt consolidations, or short-term buydown strategies and say: "I'm not calling because today's market is amazing. I'm calling because I want your file positioned so we can move fast if the math improves." That keeps the refi conversation credible and active without overselling rate relief that is not here yet.

One rookie mistake to avoid: do not confuse a better bond morning or one soft quote on an app with a trend change. In this market, one calmer headline can help for a few hours and disappear by lunch. If you tell a borrower, "rates are coming down now," and the market reverses after the Fed, you lose trust fast.

One advanced angle: use the market split between softer confidence and still-firm home prices to reposition hesitant buyers. The angle is not that homes are suddenly cheap. The angle is that weaker consumer confidence can reduce urgency and competition at the margin even while supply stays tight. For an experienced LO, that means the pitch is not "wait for cheaper rates." It is "use today's negotiating window, then preserve future refinance flexibility."

Borrower talk tracks: "You do not need the lowest rate in America. You need a payment and strategy you can live with." "If you're buying in the next month, I care more about protecting your transaction than gambling on one Fed headline." "If rates improve later, we can attack the financing. If the right house disappears now, we cannot refinance that opportunity back into existence." "An online quote is a headline. A loan strategy is a decision."

One concrete borrower example: imagine a first-time buyer under contract at $425,000 who is tight on monthly payment and closing in three weeks. In this market, the safer advice is usually to lock, confirm the real monthly payment, and see if a seller credit or structure change does more for affordability than hoping for an eighth lower in rate. That is the teachable point for new LOs and the sales point for veterans: structure beats speculation when the clock is short.

One thing to watch: if today's Fed statement is technically unchanged but the tone is hawkish, mortgage pricing can still worsen. Likewise, if the Fed sounds restrained but tomorrow's PCE runs hot, any relief may not last. So watch not just the decision, but whether the market comes away thinking inflation risk is getting more embedded.

Coaching close for the team: today is a day to sound steadier than the market. Translate the volatility, do not perform it. Protect near-term deals, use payment language with buyers, keep the refi bench warm with planning-based outreach, and make every conversation feel simpler after you speak.
