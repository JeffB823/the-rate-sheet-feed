# The Rate Sheet | Sunday, July 26, 2026

Market move: Mortgage pricing finished Friday, July 24, with a friendlier tone after bond markets improved into the close. The 10-year Treasury closed near 4.34%, down from roughly 4.37% on Thursday, and Mortgage News Daily said retail mortgage rates ended the week at their best levels since early April. Freddie Mac's weekly survey, released Thursday, July 23, put the average 30-year fixed at 6.72%, down from 6.75% the week before.

Why it matters: We are not talking about a huge rate break. We are talking about the market stepping back from the worst levels and giving borrowers proof that rates can improve when the data cools and bonds catch a bid. That changes the conversation from "rates are just high" to "the market is still volatile, but relief can show up quickly."

What this means in plain English: A better bond market does not mean every borrower suddenly gets a dramatically lower quote. It means the pressure eased. For some borrowers, that can be the difference between "this payment feels out of reach" and "let's take another look."

What to say to customers: "Rates improved a bit to end the week, so this is one of those moments where a small market move can reopen an option that did not work a few days ago. If you were close before, it is worth rerunning today."

Lock-or-float guidance: If the borrower is inside 15 days, this is still a lock-leaning market. Next week brings the July 28-29 FOMC meeting, the Fed statement on Wednesday, July 29 at 2:00 p.m. Eastern, Chair Powell at 2:30 p.m. Eastern, and inflation data on Friday, July 31 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. That is too much event risk for a borrower who cannot absorb a worse payment. If the borrower has 30 days or more and strong payment flexibility, floating can still make sense, but only with a very clear walk-away level.

Key events and times: Wednesday, July 29 at 2:00 p.m. Eastern is the Fed statement. Wednesday, July 29 at 2:30 p.m. Eastern is Powell's press conference. Friday, July 31 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern brings Personal Income and Outlays, including PCE inflation, and the Employment Cost Index. Those are the events that can either extend this bond rally or shut it down fast.

Market and housing context: Friday's June new-home-sales report showed sales running at a 627,000 annual pace, above May's revised 623,000. That is not a booming housing market. It is a market that is still functioning even with affordability pressure. For loan officers, that matters because it says buyers are still moving when the payment works and the property is right. On sentiment, the University of Michigan's preliminary July reading held at 54.4. Consumers are not optimistic. They are cautious, payment-sensitive, and quick to pause when monthly costs feel stretched.

Competitive context for online quote objections: When a borrower says, "I found something lower online," do not fight the headline rate. Move the conversation to structure and certainty. "A teaser quote is not a loan strategy. The real question is what survives underwriting, what your cash to close looks like, and whether that payment still makes sense after all the details are real."

Pipeline play, including the refi angle: This is a strong weekend to rework the near-miss file and the old refinance lead that stopped penciling. You are not calling to promise a miracle. You are calling because modest market improvement plus fresh credits, seller help, or a slightly different structure can turn a dead lead into a live conversation. The refi angle is simple: "I am not calling because rates crashed. I am calling because the market improved enough that it is worth checking whether your current loan still has room for a monthly savings, term change, or debt-consolidation win."

One rookie mistake to avoid: Do not oversell a small rate improvement like it is a trend. If you create urgency with the wrong story and Monday reverses, you lose credibility. Sell the opportunity to review, not the fantasy that the market is suddenly easy.

One advanced angle: For experienced originators, the sharper read is that buyers do not need a market victory lap; they need permission to re-engage. Use this pullback in yields to create contrast. "The market is not cheap, but it is less punitive than it was. If your client was almost there, this is when precision beats waiting."

Borrower talk tracks: "You do not need perfect rates to make a smart move. You need a payment, cash position, and plan that fit your life." "This week's market move may not sound dramatic, but in mortgage math, small improvements can matter." "If you are comparing online quotes, let's compare the full loan, not just the prettiest number on page one."

One concrete borrower example: Think about a first-time buyer who was $85 to $125 short on comfort earlier in the week. Friday's better bond tone by itself may not solve that. But pair it with a small seller concession, a different MI structure, or a slightly adjusted price point, and now the borrower has a real path instead of another delay.

One thing to watch: Watch whether next week's Fed and inflation data confirm a cooling story or remind the market that inflation is still sticky. If bonds keep improving after Wednesday, the market will start testing whether this move has legs. If not, this could end up being just a temporary exhale.

Coaching close for the team: Lead with interpretation this week. Borrowers do not need a bond trader. They need someone who can explain why the market moved, what it means for their payment, and what the next smart step is today.
