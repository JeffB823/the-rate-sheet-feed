## The Rate Sheet Script for July 18, 2026

Here’s your rate sheet for Saturday, July 18th.

The market move is this: mortgage pricing finished Friday a little better than it started the week, but not better enough to call it a real trend change. Mortgage News Daily had the average 30-year fixed at 6.63% Friday afternoon, down five basis points on the day, while the 10-year Treasury closed around 4.55%. That is relief, not victory. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey still showed the 30-year fixed average at 6.55% for the week, the highest reading in nearly a year.

Why that matters is simple. We got some help from cooler inflation data earlier in the week, and bonds stabilized a bit on Friday, but mortgage rates are still hanging near the top of the 2026 range. So if you are talking to borrowers, the message is not “rates are dropping.” The message is “the market found a little footing, but financing is still expensive and the window can close fast if inflation or oil turns back up.”

What this means in plain English: payments did not suddenly get cheap again. What improved is the tone. We moved from “getting worse in a hurry” to “a little less bad at the end of the week.” For borrowers, that means urgency still matters. For loan officers, that means you can speak from stability instead of panic, but you should not overpromise a rally that has not happened.

What to say to customers is this: “We got a little market improvement going into the weekend, but we’re still operating in a high-rate environment. If the house works and the payment works, I would not wait around for a miracle headline. I’d rather help you make a smart move now and keep the refinance plan ready for later.”

Lock-or-float guidance: if the borrower is inside 15 to 21 days, this is still a lock market. If they are inside 30 days and the deal is payment-sensitive, I would lean lock unless they have strong tolerance for weekend and geopolitical risk. If they are 45 days out and well-qualified, floating can be reasonable, but only if you have already explained that Friday’s improvement was a bounce inside a still-elevated rate range, not the start of a clean downtrend.

Key events and times for next week, all Eastern: Monday at 10:00 a.m. we get Leading Economic Indicators. Thursday at 8:30 a.m. we get jobless claims. Friday is the bigger one for rates, with S&P flash services PMI and manufacturing PMI at 9:45 a.m. and new home sales at 10:00 a.m. Also keep the July 28 to July 29 FOMC meeting on the horizon, because the market is already starting to think ahead to Fed tone even before the meeting arrives.

Market and housing context: June housing starts jumped, but the headline was helped by multifamily. Single-family starts were basically flat to down, and single-family permits slipped again. Builder confidence also fell to 34 in July, and buyer traffic stayed weak. That tells you the housing market is still moving, but it is not moving with conviction. On the consumer side, University of Michigan sentiment improved to 54.4 in preliminary July data, so people feel a little less rattled than they did a month ago, but they still do not feel good. In other words, housing supply is uneven, builders are cautious, and borrowers are slightly less discouraged without being truly confident.

Competitive context for online quote objections: when a borrower says, “I saw something lower online,” do not race to defend your rate sheet. Slow the conversation down. Say, “That headline quote may be real for somebody, but it usually assumes perfect credit, a strong down payment, ideal loan size, and timing that may not match your file. My job is not to win a screenshot contest. My job is to show you the payment, cash to close, and strategy that actually gets you to the table.” In this market, clarity beats rate shopping theater.

Pipeline play, and this needs the refi angle: stop treating refinance as a dead conversation just because rate-and-term payment drops are harder today. The refi play right now is segmentation. Call past clients with FHA loans carrying monthly MI, VA borrowers who may benefit from an IRRRL later, cash-out prospects buried in high-rate credit card debt, and 2024 to 2026 buyers who need a future-review plan, not a false promise. The script is: “I’m not calling to force a refinance today. I’m calling to map the trigger point where a refinance starts making sense for you so we can move fast when the market gives us the shot.”

One rookie mistake to avoid: do not confuse a better Friday with a safer market. Newer LOs get into trouble when they hear “rates improved” and translate that into “we should wait.” Improvement after a bad week is not the same thing as a durable trend.

One advanced angle: use the disconnect between slightly better consumer sentiment and still-weak housing traffic as a sales advantage. Borrowers may feel emotionally better than they did in June, but many are still delaying action. That creates room for your serious buyers to negotiate with motivated sellers while weaker buyers sit on the fence. Veteran LOs can frame today’s market as expensive financing, yes, but also less crowded competition.

Borrower talk tracks:

“If you’re waiting for perfect rates, you may miss the right house and still not get the payment you want.”

“We can solve for the monthly payment today and stay ready to improve the debt structure later.”

“The biggest mistake in this market is comparing your real payment to someone else’s ad.”

“You do not need the lowest rate in America. You need the right loan for your timeline, cash position, and plan.”

One concrete borrower example: say you have a first-time buyer looking at a $425,000 home who is nervous because the payment feels stretched. The wrong move is to tell them to wait for the Fed. The better move is to show three paths: buy now with the current payment and a refinance review target, ask for seller help to offset cash to close or payment shock, or lower the purchase target slightly to protect monthly comfort. That turns fear into options, and options create decisions.

One thing to watch: if next week’s PMI data come in hot, or if oil and broader inflation fears re-accelerate, Friday’s bond improvement can disappear fast. If the data stay tame, the market may keep trying to stabilize, but right now stabilization is the bullish case, not a major rate drop.

Coaching close for the team: your value this week is not predicting rates. Your value is translating uncertainty into a plan the borrower can act on. Be the person who makes the payment, the timing, and the next move feel clear.
