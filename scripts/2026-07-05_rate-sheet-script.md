# The Rate Sheet Script for 2026-07-05

## Market move

Good morning team. Because today is Sunday, July 5, 2026, there is no live bond-market move to react to. The real setup is the post-jobs, post-holiday-week picture we carry into Monday. Freddie Mac's weekly survey showed the 30-year fixed averaging 6.43% as of July 2, down from 6.49% the prior week, and Mortgage News Daily's daily index was 6.60% on July 2 versus 6.65% on July 1. In other words, mortgage pricing improved a little, but not enough to call this a true breakout lower.

## Why it matters

It matters because the June jobs report was soft enough to help rates, but not weak enough to give the market an all-clear signal. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported just 57,000 payrolls added in June, while unemployment held at 4.2%. That tells the bond market the economy is cooling, but not cracking. So lenders got a little relief, yet the market still has room to turn choppy again the minute traders start thinking about inflation, Treasury supply, or a Fed that does not sound ready to rush.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: the market handed us a better conversation, not a better guarantee. The borrower who was nervous on Wednesday should feel a little less squeezed today, but this is still a mid-sixes mortgage market, not a low-sixes market pretending to come back.

## What to say to customers

Say it this way: "We got a little help from the bond market, so this is a better moment to make a decision than it was a few days ago. But I would not confuse a little improvement with a major trend change. If the payment works now, we should act from strength instead of hoping the market gifts us more."

## Lock-or-float guidance

For closings inside 15 days, lock. For 15 to 30 days, I still lean lock unless the borrower has real flexibility and understands that Monday's open can reverse some of this improvement quickly. Float only when the file has margin, the borrower has patience, and you have already explained the risk in plain language. This is still a market where discipline travels better than hope.

## Market and housing context

Housing is not dead. It is selective, payment-sensitive, and increasingly negotiable. The MBA's July 1 release showed purchase applications up 1% week over week and 3% from a year earlier, which tells you buyers are still moving when the math starts to cooperate. Redfin's July 2 weekly update said the median U.S. housing payment posted its first year-over-year increase since October, even as pending sales still rose 2% from a year ago. Realtor.com's June housing report showed asking prices down 2.5% year over year, new listings up 2.4%, and pending sales up 3.7%. That is your backdrop: affordability is still tight, but the market is giving borrowers more negotiating room than many of them realize.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "An online lender showed me a lower rate," do not argue with the screenshot. Reframe the decision. "A headline quote is not the same thing as a real strategy. Let's compare the actual rate, cost, lender credit, cash to close, and certainty to close on your exact file." In a market like this, the cheapest-looking quote often wins the browser tab and loses the transaction.

## Pipeline play

Today's pipeline play is simple: work both the purchase lane and the refi lane on purpose. On purchase, call borrowers who paused because the payment felt just out of reach and revisit seller credits, price reductions, and temporary buydown math. On refi, this is the required angle: do not wait for a mythical refi wave. Hunt monthly-payment improvement now. Look for FHA borrowers with stronger equity profiles, conventional borrowers nearing MI removal, cash-out files that consolidate higher-rate debt, and buyers from the last 12 to 18 months who never got a serious post-close mortgage review. The refi play is not "rates crashed." The refi play is "your current structure may no longer be your best structure."

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "The jobs report was weak, so rates should keep falling." That is lazy and it creates waiters. The cleaner version is: "We did get some relief from weaker labor data, but mortgage rates still depend on what bonds, inflation expectations, and the Fed do next."

## One advanced angle

Here is the veteran angle: if this week's softer payroll number cannot produce a cleaner rally in mortgage pricing, that tells you the market still does not trust the path lower. That usually means spread behavior, Treasury supply, and Fed tone matter as much as the headline economic miss. If Wednesday's minutes read firmer than expected or if Treasury auctions come in soft, lenders can stay stubborn even without a hot inflation print.

## Borrower talk tracks

"You do not need the perfect market to make a smart mortgage move. You need a payment, a structure, and a backup plan that still make sense if rates bounce next week."

"The market is finally giving buyers a little more negotiating room. Sometimes the smartest win is not waiting for an eighth lower in rate. It is getting the seller to help solve the payment."

"If you already own, I am not calling to promise some magical refinance. I am calling to see whether we can improve cash flow, remove mortgage insurance, or clean up expensive debt with the house working harder for you."

## One concrete borrower example

Picture a borrower who bought last summer with FHA, has made every payment, and now has enough appreciation plus paydown that the monthly budget is getting squeezed more by mortgage insurance and credit-card debt than by the note rate itself. Your conversation is: "I am not calling because the market suddenly got easy. I am calling because your loan may be ready for a smarter structure. If we can reduce MI or roll expensive revolving debt into a controlled payment, that can move your monthly life even if the new rate is not dramatically lower."

## One thing to watch

Watch Wednesday, July 8 at 2:00 p.m. ET when the FOMC minutes hit. If the minutes show a committee still more worried about inflation than growth, the market may treat this jobs report as helpful but not decisive. If the tone sounds more concerned about cooling demand, bonds have room to improve.

## Coaching close

This week, do not try to sound like a rate predictor. Sound like the clearest person in the room. Explain what improved, explain what did not, and turn that into one confident purchase call and one thoughtful refi call before Monday afternoon.
