# The Rate Sheet Script for Saturday, August 1, 2026

Good morning team. Here is your Saturday, August 1, 2026 Rate Sheet. The market move is this: rates finished Friday moving the wrong way again. Mortgage News Daily closed its 30-year fixed index at 6.83%, up 6 basis points on the day, and the site flagged MBS prices as significantly weaker. In Treasuries, the official Friday close from the U.S. Treasury showed the 10-year at 4.75% and the 30-year at 5.27%. So even though we are heading into a weekend, the message from the market is not calm. It is pressure.

Why it matters is that Friday gave the bond market another reason to stay defensive. The Employment Cost Index came in at up 0.9% for the quarter and up 3.4% year over year. That is not a runaway wage story, but it is still firm enough to keep inflation nerves alive. Layer that on top of Wednesday's Fed hold at 3.50% to 3.75%, with a 9 to 3 vote and three dissents for a hike, and you get the real takeaway: the Fed did not move, but the market still thinks inflation risk is very much on the table.

What this means in plain English: mortgage rates are acting like long-term inflation is still the bigger problem. Newer LOs should hear it this way: the Fed controls overnight money, but mortgage rates live more in the bond market, and the bond market is still not convinced inflation is fully beaten. Veterans can say it more sharply: this is a credibility and term-premium story now, and long-duration borrowing costs are staying elevated until the market sees cleaner evidence that inflation and wage pressure are rolling over.

What to say to customers: "The market ended the week with higher long-term rates, so this is not one of those weekends where we expect some magical Monday improvement. Mortgage pricing is still being pushed around by inflation concerns more than by headlines about the Fed simply standing still. If the home and payment work now, we should make a decision based on today's real market, not a hoped-for reset."

Lock-or-float guidance: for closings inside 15 days, lock. For closings inside 30 days, the bias is still lock unless the borrower is truly flexible and can handle volatility. For borrowers beyond 45 days, a cautious float is still defensible, but only with tight follow-up because next week brings data that can move rates fast. The market is entering the new week with a bearish tone, so floating is a strategy decision, not a default.

Key events and times: Monday, August 3, 2026, ISM Manufacturing for July is due at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. Tuesday, August 4, 2026, U.S. trade data for June is due at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Wednesday, August 5, 2026, MBA applications hit at 7:00 a.m. Eastern and ISM Services is due at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. Friday, August 7, 2026, the July jobs report lands at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. That Friday report is the big one, but Monday and Wednesday can absolutely shape the tone before payrolls.

Market and housing context: housing is still dealing with affordability fatigue, not a demand rebound. Freddie Mac's weekly survey on Thursday pushed the average 30-year fixed to 6.66%, the highest level in a year. Mortgage Bankers Association data released Wednesday showed applications down 6.4% week over week. At the same time, final July University of Michigan consumer sentiment improved to 55.2 from 49.5 in June, which tells you consumers feel a little less awful than they did a month ago, but they are still far from comfortable. That is a useful combo to understand: people may be stabilizing emotionally, but higher rates are still choking action.

Competitive context for online quote objections: this is where we need to slow borrowers down. If someone says, "I saw something much lower online," the answer is: "You probably saw a headline rate built around points, a very specific scenario, or a snapshot that moved before the page even loaded for you. What matters is the real cost for your credit profile, your timeline, and your cash-to-close." Use Friday's move as proof that mortgage pricing can change meaningfully even when consumers are just heading into the weekend.

Pipeline play, with the required refi angle: make this a weekend database touch day. Reach out to purchase leads who stalled over payment shock and to past clients with FHA, recent builder buydowns, or high consumer debt. The refi angle is not "rates are dropping now." The refi angle is "let's update your file now so when a window opens, you are positioned before the crowd." That keeps you credible and keeps dormant leads attached to you instead of drifting back to whichever website pings them next.

One rookie mistake to avoid: do not tell borrowers that because consumer sentiment improved, buyers are about to come flooding back in and rates should settle down. Sentiment improved from a very weak level. That is not the same thing as affordability improving, and it is definitely not the same thing as mortgage pricing improving.

One advanced angle: use the split between softer consumer mood and still-firm wage pressure to tailor your pitch. With first-time buyers, lead with payment clarity and options. With higher-income move-up buyers, talk about negotiating power, seller concessions, and future refinance optionality. Same market, different pressure points. The LO who wins is the one who translates the same macro story into the right borrower language.

Borrower talk tracks: "Rates are being driven more by the bond market than by the Fed standing still." "An online quote is a headline; your mortgage decision is a full payment strategy." "If buying now solves the life need, we can structure smart terms today and stay ready to improve them later." "The best plan is not guessing the perfect week. It is making a good decision and keeping future options open."

One concrete borrower example: imagine a borrower under contract at $525,000 who planned to float one more week because they saw a flashy ad online. Friday's market gave you the coaching moment. You can say, "This week the bond market pushed mortgage costs higher even without a Fed hike. If we lock now, we protect the payment you already qualified for. If rates improve later, we can revisit options, but if we wait and pricing worsens, your monthly payment gets harder to defend." That is practical, calm, and real.

One thing to watch: Monday's ISM Manufacturing report. If price pressures or overall activity come in hotter than expected, the market may carry Friday's bearish tone straight into the new week. If the data are softer, bonds might finally get a little breathing room. Either way, Monday at 10:00 a.m. Eastern is the first real test of whether this selloff is extending or pausing.

Coaching close for the team: speak with conviction, but not with prediction. The market ended the week under pressure. Your job is to explain why, translate it into borrower choices, and keep every purchase conversation connected to a future refinance plan without sounding like you're selling hope. Be the calm interpreter, not the rate fortune teller.
