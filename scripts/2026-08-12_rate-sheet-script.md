# The Rate Sheet Script

Wednesday, August 12, 2026

Recording note: This script is written before the 8:30 a.m. ET CPI release.

**Market move**

Good morning. The rate story this morning is pretty simple: mortgage pricing is still under pressure because the bond market came into today defensive, not relaxed. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed average was 6.79% on August 11 after 6.80% on August 10, so consumer-facing rates are still sitting near the high end of the recent range, not meaningfully recovering. Under the surface, the bigger issue is that the 10-year Treasury closed at 4.72% on August 10 versus 4.65% on August 7, and the 2-year moved up to 4.25% from 4.19%. That tells you the market has been pricing in more inflation risk and less confidence that the Fed gets an easy glide path.

**Why it matters**

When Treasuries back up like that, mortgage-backed securities usually do not get a free pass. Even if the retail mortgage rate headline looks flat from one day to the next, lender pricing tends to stay cautious because bond traders are demanding more yield to hold duration. In plain terms, the market is still charging a penalty for uncertainty, and that uncertainty is centered on inflation, energy, and Fed policy.

**What this means in plain English**

Here is the easy version for newer loan officers: rates are not high this morning because lenders woke up in a bad mood. Rates are high because investors still are not convinced inflation is cooling fast enough, and when investors worry about inflation, they make long-term borrowing more expensive. So the mortgage market is trading like it needs proof before it offers relief.

**What to say to customers**

You can say: "The market isn't waiting on a lender decision today. It's waiting on inflation data. Until the bond market sees cleaner evidence that inflation is calming down, mortgage pricing is likely to stay choppy and a little defensive."

That line works because it explains the why without overloading the borrower with market jargon.

**Lock-or-float guidance**

For today, the default posture is defensive into the CPI release. If a borrower is close to closing, payment sensitive, or already uncomfortable with current numbers, locking before the data is the cleaner play. If a borrower has time, strong tolerance for volatility, and the file does not need to be priced this morning, floating is really a bet on CPI coming in soft enough to help bonds. That is a real possibility, but call it what it is: an event-driven gamble, not a strategy.

**Key events and times**

The biggest event is the July Consumer Price Index at 8:30 a.m. ET on Wednesday, August 12, 2026. After that, watch the 10-year Treasury auction at 1:00 p.m. ET because weak demand there would reinforce the message that long-end rates still need to stay elevated. Then tomorrow, Thursday, August 13, 2026, the Producer Price Index hits at 8:30 a.m. ET, so even a good CPI print today would not fully end the inflation conversation.

**Market and housing context**

Housing is still giving borrowers mixed signals. Freddie Mac's weekly survey showed the 30-year fixed averaging 6.69% for the week of August 6, up from 6.66% the week before. Fannie Mae's latest weekly application data for the week ending August 7 showed purchase application dollar volume up just 0.1% week over week, while refinance dollar volume jumped 7.8%. That is an important split. Purchase demand is hanging in, but not accelerating. Refi interest wakes up fast anytime consumers think there might be even a small opening.

On the resale side, Realtor.com reported on August 11 that July existing home sales slipped 1.7% from June to a 4.06 million annualized pace, though they were still up 0.7% from a year earlier. That tells you the market is not dead, but affordability is still acting like a ceiling.

**Competitive context for online quote objections**

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," do not argue with the screenshot. Reframe it. Say: "You may have seen a best-case headline quote captured at a different moment in the bond market, with points, assumptions, or credit details that may not match your file. The more useful question is what today's live execution looks like for your scenario." In this kind of market, stale screenshots age fast. Your edge is not pretending every quote is fake. Your edge is helping the borrower compare real terms, real timing, and real certainty.

**Pipeline play with required refi angle**

Today's pipeline play is a refi touch, even if the borrower is not obviously in the money. The Fannie data matters here: refi application volume moved 7.8% higher week over week, and rate-term refi volume rose 6.9%. That is the market telling you consumers are still willing to engage when there is even a modest opening. So do not wait for the perfect rate headline. Re-open old conversations with a simple angle: "I don't need this to be your forever rate to make the math work. I just need to know if today's payment, cash flow, or debt reset is worth a five-minute review."

**One rookie mistake to avoid**

Do not tell borrowers, "The Fed doesn't control mortgage rates," and stop there. That is technically true and practically useless. The better version is: "The Fed does not set your 30-year mortgage rate directly, but Fed policy and inflation expectations absolutely move the bond market that mortgage rates follow." Teach the full idea, not the half-truth.

**One advanced angle**

Here is the sharper angle for veterans: the real problem is not just the level of Treasury yields, it is the market's reluctance to price a lower term premium before inflation risk is clearly contained. If CPI is only "not terrible" instead of clearly soft, mortgages may not improve much because the market still has oil risk, supply pressure from Treasury issuance, and a Fed that cannot sound relaxed yet. That is why some days a decent data print helps the screen more than it helps the rate sheet.

**Borrower talk tracks**

Say this to a purchase borrower: "Inventory is better than it was, but affordability still matters more than selection. If the home works and the payment works, waiting for a perfect rate could cost you more than refinancing later."

Say this to a cash-out borrower: "The right question isn't whether today's rate is your dream rate. It's whether this solves a more expensive problem you're carrying right now."

Say this to a rate-term refi borrower: "If the market gives us a dip, I want your file ready before everyone else rushes in. Prepared borrowers capture windows. Curious borrowers usually miss them."

**One concrete borrower example**

Think about a first-time buyer who got discouraged two weeks ago when rates backed up and decided to pause. Today's coaching point is not, "Rates are about to crash." It is, "The market is volatile, inventory is somewhat better, and if CPI helps bonds later today we want you positioned to act instead of starting from scratch." That turns market commentary into action.

**One thing to watch**

Watch how the bond market reacts after CPI, not just the CPI headline itself. If inflation data comes in soft and the 10-year yield still refuses to rally much, that is the market telling you the rate floor is still sticky. If CPI is soft and bonds improve cleanly, that is your signal to get more aggressive on same-day borrower outreach.

**Coaching close**

Lead with interpretation today. Borrowers do not need a lecture on macroeconomics. They need a calm guide who can explain why rates are sticky, what could change by mid-morning, and what action makes sense for their file right now.
