# The Rate Sheet Script for Tuesday, August 11, 2026

Good morning. The market move today is basically a pause with tension under the surface. Treasuries closed Monday a touch higher on the long end, with the 10-year Treasury at about 4.04% versus 4.01% on Friday, and mortgage rates are still sitting near their highest levels of 2026. That means we are not waking up to a big rally we can celebrate, but we are also not looking at a fresh selloff. The bond market is in wait-and-see mode ahead of tomorrow morning's inflation report.

Why that matters is simple. Mortgage pricing does not need a Fed meeting to move. It moves when the bond market decides inflation is getting better or worse. Right now, traders are trying to figure out whether Friday's weak jobs report matters more, or whether sticky inflation still wins. If tomorrow's CPI comes in cool, rates have room to improve. If it comes in hot, the market can quickly start repricing for higher-for-longer again.

In plain English, today is not the day to promise better pricing tomorrow. Today is the day to explain that the market is balanced on one report. We are close enough to the edge that one inflation print can change the tone fast.

What to say to customers is this: "We're not seeing a major break lower yet, but the market is trying to decide if inflation is finally cooling enough to help rates. That answer probably starts showing up tomorrow morning. My job is to help you make a smart decision before the market makes it for you."

Lock-or-float guidance today should stay practical. If the borrower is inside 15 days, has thin savings, or would be upset by even a modest rate setback, locking still makes sense. If the borrower has time, margin, and understands that tomorrow's CPI at 8:30 a.m. Eastern can swing pricing either way, a disciplined float can be reasonable. This is not a casual float. It is an event-driven float with a clear deadline.

Key events and times: today the market mostly trades on positioning, then tomorrow, Wednesday, August 12, 2026, we get July CPI at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Thursday, August 13, 2026, we get PPI at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Friday brings retail sales, and that will matter because a strong consumer can keep inflation pressure alive even when housing is soft.

The bigger market and housing context is mixed. Freddie Mac's latest weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed at 6.69%, up from 6.66% the week before. Redfin's latest housing update shows pending sales down 3.7% week over week to a five-month low, while new listings are still creeping higher. That is the backdrop you need to explain well: affordability is pressuring demand, but more inventory is giving buyers a little more leverage. In other words, the payment is still the problem, but negotiation is improving.

That leads right into the competitive context when a borrower throws out an online quote objection. The right answer is not to fight over an eighth. The right answer is: "A screen quote is not a strategy. The real question is whether that quote is locked, whether the costs are real, whether the loan structure fits your timeline, and whether someone is managing the risk around tomorrow's inflation report." In this market, execution is part of the price.

Pipeline play for today, and this one needs the refi angle: call every near-miss purchase borrower and every closed client from the last 18 months who took a rate above today's market. For purchase leads, position the conversation around payment control, seller concessions, and renegotiation power in a slower market. For past clients, do not lead with "rates dropped," because they really have not. Lead with payment strategy. Ask whether monthly cash flow, debt consolidation, mortgage insurance removal, or shortening the term could create a win even before a true refi wave arrives. The refi angle today is not "everyone should refinance now." It is "let's identify the few borrowers who still have a math-based reason to act."

One rookie mistake to avoid is sounding too certain about tomorrow. Do not say, "CPI should help rates." That is a guess dressed up as advice. Say, "Tomorrow is a major volatility event, and we are making today's lock decision with that risk in mind."

One advanced angle for veteran producers: separate Fed talk from mortgage-rate talk on borrower calls. If a customer says, "I thought the Fed was supposed to cut," explain that mortgages care more about inflation expectations and bond yields than the last headline about the Fed. That sharper explanation builds trust because it matches what borrowers are actually seeing in the market.

Borrower talk tracks for today:

"If you need certainty, we can buy certainty today with a lock."

"If you want to float into tomorrow's CPI, let's do it on purpose, not by default."

"Even in a high-rate market, buyers can win when more listings create room for credits, repairs, and negotiation."

"The cheapest quote is not always the lowest-cost decision if it comes with weak execution or surprise fees."

One concrete borrower example: say you have a first-time buyer who got spooked by rate headlines and an online lender flashing a slightly lower teaser. The talk track is, "You are not buying a headline, and you are not marrying this rate forever. What matters today is whether the home, payment, and cash-to-close work now. If tomorrow helps, great. If it hurts, we protected you or we knew the risk we were taking."

One thing to watch is whether tomorrow's core CPI lands closer to 0.2% month over month or 0.3%. That difference sounds small, but in this market it can be the difference between relief and renewed pressure.

Close with this coaching note for the team: do not try to sound smartest by predicting the market. Sound strongest by translating the market. Borrowers do not need a bond trader. They need a calm guide who can explain what changed, what it means, and what the next smart move is.
