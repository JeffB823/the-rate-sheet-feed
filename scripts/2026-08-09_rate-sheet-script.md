# The Rate Sheet Script | August 9, 2026

**Market move**

Here is the setup for Sunday, August 9. The last real market move came Friday, August 7, and it finally helped us a little. After a much weaker-than-expected jobs report, bonds improved, the 10-year Treasury finished around 4.65%, and Mortgage News Daily showed the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.74%, down from 6.77%. That is not a huge rally, but it did stop the late-July rate push higher and gave the market a little breathing room going into next week.

**Why it matters**

Why that matters is simple: the market is trying to decide which story is bigger right now, slowing growth or sticky inflation. Friday’s jobs report favored the slowing-growth story. The Labor Department showed payrolls down 23,000 in July, with another 103,000 jobs revised out of May and June. When growth data softens like that, bond traders usually pull back from the idea that the Fed has to keep tightening aggressively, and mortgage pricing can improve.

**What this means in plain English**

In plain English, the economy looked weaker, so rates got a little better. Not because lenders felt generous, and not because the Fed cut anything. Investors simply got a little less convinced that inflation pressure alone will keep pushing long-term rates higher every week. For borrowers, that means the payment picture improved a touch, but affordability is still tight and this is still a cautious market.

**What to say to customers**

The borrower version sounds like this: "We got some relief, not a reset. The market gave us a slightly better window, but it did not erase the payment challenge. If the home still fits your budget and goals, this is the kind of market where structure and negotiation matter as much as the rate itself." That keeps the conversation practical instead of dramatic.

**Lock-or-float guidance**

Lock guidance is still mostly defensive. If the borrower is inside 30 days, if the payment already works, or if the file is sensitive to even a small pricing hit, locking still makes the most sense. Floating only gets more reasonable when the borrower has time, cash cushion, and a clear reason to bet that next week’s inflation data comes in friendly. The market improved on growth fears. It still has to get through inflation data without giving that improvement back.

**Key events and times**

The calendar matters this week. Monday, August 10 at 2:00 p.m. Eastern, the Fed releases the Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey. Tuesday, August 11 at 10:00 a.m. Eastern, Fannie Mae is scheduled to publish weekly purchase and refi application data for the week ending August 7. Wednesday, August 12 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern is CPI. Thursday, August 13 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern is PPI. Those are the checkpoints that can decide whether Friday’s improvement sticks or disappears.

**Market and housing context**

The bigger housing backdrop still helps good originators. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey has the 30-year fixed averaging 6.69% as of August 6, the highest in about a year. At the same time, Realtor.com says median list prices were down 2.4% year over year in July, and 20% of listings had price cuts. Redfin also showed pending sales falling 3.7% week over week, the steepest drop since 2022. So yes, rates are still a problem, but buyers are seeing more room to negotiate on the house side. That matters because affordability can improve through credits, pricing, and terms even when rate sheets do not improve much.

**Competitive context for online quote objections**

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," do not argue with the headline rate. Slow it down and compare execution. Ask if that quote is locked, whether it includes points, what credit score and LTV assumptions were used, whether mortgage insurance is quoted correctly, and whether the payment includes real taxes and insurance. The clean line is: "If it is a real quote, we can compare it line by line. If it is a marketing rate built on perfect assumptions, that is not the same as a loan you can actually close."

**Pipeline play with required refi angle**

Today’s pipeline play is a purchase-plus-refi split. On the purchase side, re-open shoppers who backed away during the late-July rate spike and tell them the market gave us enough of a break to rerun payments with seller-credit strategy in mind. On the refi side, stay targeted. This is not broad blast-refi season. It is a tactical refi market for FHA borrowers who may be able to remove MI, VA borrowers who could benefit from an IRRRL later if this rate improvement grows, debt-consolidation cash-out files where the total monthly picture still improves, and past clients whose loan structure can be upgraded before rates are truly attractive again.

**One rookie mistake to avoid**

The rookie mistake is calling this a turnaround. It is not. A one-day improvement after weak jobs data is just that. If you oversell it now and CPI comes in hot on Wednesday, you sound like you do not understand your own market. Say "better," not "back."

**One advanced angle**

The advanced angle is this: consumer mood is still fragile even as the market catches a small break. The New York Fed’s July Survey of Consumer Expectations, released August 7, showed one-year inflation expectations ticking down to 3.6% and future credit availability improving, but unemployment expectations also moved higher. That is an important mix. Borrowers may sound optimistic for five minutes when they hear rates improved, but they are still nervous about jobs, cash flow, and making a wrong move. Veteran LOs should speak to both sides at once: payment opportunity and decision anxiety.

**Borrower talk tracks**

Use lines like these. "You do not need a perfect rate to make a smart move. You need a payment, a cash-to-close number, and a plan that still works if rates stay choppy." Or: "In this market, the monthly payment is only half the strategy. The other half is what we can negotiate from the seller." And for rate shoppers: "A lower advertised rate is only better if the loan actually closes that way."

**One concrete borrower example**

Think about a first-time buyer looking at a $450,000 home who paused two weeks ago because the payment felt too close for comfort. The move today is not to say, "Good news, rates are falling." The move is to say, "The market improved a little, and sellers are still cutting prices in a lot of places. Let’s rerun the payment with a modest rate improvement, then test what a seller credit or buydown would do. If the payment works after that, we have a path. If it does not, we know exactly what has to change." That sounds like advice, not sales pressure.

**One thing to watch**

One thing to watch is whether Wednesday’s CPI validates Friday’s bond rally. If inflation comes in cooler than feared, this market may build on the improvement. If inflation runs hot, Friday’s bounce will look temporary and rate sheets can worsen quickly.

**Coaching close for the team**

The coaching close is simple. This week, do not try to sound brilliant. Try to sound clear. Explain that the market gave borrowers a little help, explain what could change that by Wednesday morning, and keep the conversation centered on payment strategy instead of rate theater. That is how you earn trust in a market like this.
