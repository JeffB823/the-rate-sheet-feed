# The Rate Sheet Script for September 3, 2026

This script was prepared at approximately `6:32 a.m. Eastern` on Thursday, September 3, 2026. The `8:30 a.m. ET` weekly jobless claims report, the `8:30 a.m. ET` speech from Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller, and the `10:00 a.m. ET` ISM Services report were not out at the time of writing, so this is a pre-data market read.

## Market move

Good morning, team. The market starts today with a small breath of relief, but not a real recovery. Mortgage News Daily's daily index closed Wednesday, September 2 at `6.91%` for the top-tier 30-year fixed, up another `2` basis points and into the highest range we have seen in more than a year. This morning, bond markets are a little better. Treasury yields are off yesterday's highs, and Mortgage News Daily shows MBS improving slightly in early trading. So the setup is this: pricing got worse yesterday, and today opens a little calmer, but still at elevated levels.

## Why it matters

What matters is that mortgage rates are not starting from neutral. They are starting from damage already done. The bond market had been selling off on inflation nerves, oil pressure, and a market that still thinks the Fed may need to stay tighter for longer. This morning's slight rally looks more like a pause than a pivot. That means one decent morning does not erase the broader pressure that pushed many real-world borrowers back to seeing rates with a `7` in front of them.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: the market may be a little less ugly this morning, but money is still expensive. If today's data comes in soft, rates could steady or improve a bit. If the data says the economy is still running hot enough to keep inflation sticky, lenders can stay defensive fast. Borrowers should hear that the market is calmer, not cured.

## What to say to customers

Say this: "The market is giving us a little more breathing room this morning, but rates are still near the high end of this year's range. I want to help you make a decision that works even if the next headline moves pricing again."

If they ask why rates are still high, say: "The bond market still wants proof that inflation and the economy are cooling enough. Until investors believe that more clearly, mortgage rates can stay touchy even on better mornings."

## Lock-or-float guidance

This is still a lock-bias setup for borrowers inside `21` days, anyone close to qualifying limits, and anyone whose comfort level depends on today's payment holding together. The market is calmer, but today's calendar is too important to confuse calm with safety.

The float case is still the same borrower profile as always: more time, more reserves, more payment cushion, and a willingness to accept event risk. But be precise with that advice. Floating into a morning with jobless claims, a Fed speech, and ISM Services is an active bet.

## Market and housing context

Yesterday gave us two stories at once. First, the financing story got worse. Mortgage News Daily said the average top-tier 30-year fixed moved up to `6.91%`, and it noted that many borrowers are already seeing `7%` or higher in the real world. Second, the activity story is not dead. MBA reported mortgage applications rose `0.8%` for the week ending August 28, which tells you buyers and refi shoppers have not vanished even with rates under pressure.

Housing supply is also giving buyers a little more room than the financing market is. Redfin's latest weekly update said new listings hit a four-month high and the total number of homes for sale also moved higher. That is the tradeoff worth explaining to borrowers: the rate market is tougher, but the housing market is still more negotiable than it was during the frenzy years.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

This matters more when rates are elevated because borrowers shop harder. Mortgage News Daily makes the point clearly: the headline average assumes an ideal setup, and many actual borrowers are already seeing something higher once points, fees, lock period, credit, and equity position are applied. So when a borrower sends the online screenshot, do not fight the screenshot.

Say: "That quote may be real for a very narrow scenario. My job is to show you what is real for your file, your timeline, and your cost structure."

Then say: "A low headline quote is only better if it survives disclosures, underwriting, and closing without getting more expensive."

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

Today's pipeline play is a payment-stability call block. Start with every purchase borrower who is active, conditional, or close to commitment and re-anchor them around payment, cash to close, and backup options. In this market, silence creates fallout.

The required refi angle is future positioning, not false urgency. Tell recent buyers, older database leads, and even today's purchase clients: "I am not asking you to force a refinance today. I am asking permission to put you on a refinance review list so if the market improves later, you are first in line and not starting over." That keeps the refi conversation honest and builds a reason to stay in touch.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Rates should get better after the Fed meeting." That is lazy and often wrong. Mortgage rates follow the bond market, and the bond market is reacting to inflation expectations, economic resilience, Treasury supply, oil, and risk sentiment, not just one Fed headline.

## One advanced angle

Here is the veteran angle. This is not only a Fed-path story. It is also a long-end compensation story. Even with a small rally this morning, the market is still demanding extra yield to own longer-duration risk. That is why mortgage rates can stay stubbornly high even if a few data points soften. The term-premium problem has not gone away.

## Borrower talk tracks

"We got a little breathing room this morning, but not a full reset."

"If the payment works and the house works, the question is whether you want certainty now or more market exposure for a possible improvement later."

"This is a harder financing market, but it is also a market where buyers can still negotiate."

"I cannot promise the next rate move. I can help you make the next smart move."

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a move-up buyer who can afford the home but hates the idea of locking near the top of the recent range. The weak move is to say, "Let's just wait a few days." The stronger move is to frame choices: lock now and protect the approval, negotiate for seller credit to offset cost, or float only if there is enough reserve and emotional tolerance to handle a worse quote after today's data or Friday's jobs report. That turns anxiety into a decision tree instead of a stall.

## One thing to watch

Watch whether today's data actually validates the early bond-market bounce. If jobless claims drift higher, Waller sounds less urgent about additional tightening, or ISM Services cools, rates may stabilize. If claims stay low, Waller leans hawkish, or services data runs hot, the market can quickly go back to treating yesterday's selloff as the real trend.

## One short coaching close for the team

Be the calm interpreter today. Borrowers do not need a bond trader. They need someone who can explain why the market feels better this morning without pretending the pressure is gone.
