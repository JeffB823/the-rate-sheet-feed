# The Rate Sheet Script for 2026-07-08

## Market move

Good morning team. Wednesday, July 8, 2026 is opening with mortgage pressure, not panic, but definitely some pressure. Mortgage News Daily has the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.63%, up 4 basis points from the prior survey, while live pricing shows the 10-year Treasury around 4.57% and agency MBS weaker. The immediate driver is not a big economic report. It is the bond market reacting to higher oil and a little more inflation concern as Middle East headlines pushed money out of bonds yesterday.

## Why it matters

It matters because this is the kind of move that can feel small on paper and still damage borrower psychology in real life. When rates rise because inflation risk is being repriced, lenders get less generous and borrowers get more hesitant at the same time. That is exactly when a loan officer has to become the translator instead of the quote machine.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: the market did not fall apart, but it did get a little more expensive. We are not looking at a crisis move. We are looking at a reminder that rates can drift higher even on a light data day if inflation-sensitive headlines start steering bonds. For borrowers, that means waiting is still a real decision with a real cost.

## What to say to customers

Say it this way: "Rates moved up a little, not because your file changed, but because the bond market got a little more nervous about inflation. If the payment is still workable, today is a good day to make a decision instead of hoping the next headline is kinder."

## Lock-or-float guidance

For closings inside 15 days, lock. For 15 to 30 days, I lean lock unless the borrower has clear payment cushion and understands that today has both an auction event and Fed-minutes risk. For 30-plus days, floating can still be reasonable, but only if you frame it as a managed risk decision, not a prediction. The burden of proof is on floating today.

## Market and housing context

The housing market still looks alive, but it is acting like a market that needs every dollar explained. Redfin's latest weekly update showed the median U.S. sale price at a record high and the median monthly housing payment up year over year for the first time in eight months. Pending sales still nudged higher, but that happened in a market where affordability is already stretched.

Borrower behavior looks the same way. Fannie Mae's weekly application data released yesterday showed both purchase and refinance dollar volume dropped sharply week over week in the holiday-shortened week, even though both were still up from a year ago. That tells you intent is still there, but urgency fades fast when rates or timing get less friendly. On top of that, the New York Fed's June Survey of Consumer Expectations, also released yesterday, showed short- and medium-term inflation expectations moved higher while future credit access expectations deteriorated slightly. Borrowers are not giving up. They are getting more selective.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I'm seeing lower rates online," your answer is not to debate the ad. Your answer is: "You probably are seeing a lower headline. Now let's see whether that rate assumes points, a perfect credit box, a bigger down payment, or a closing timeline that does not fit your contract." In a market like this, internet quotes win the first glance and lose the full comparison. Your job is to slow the conversation down before the borrower confuses marketing with execution.

## Pipeline play

Today's pipeline play is to call the borrower who almost moved last week and went quiet when payment got tight. The script is simple: "The market reminded us today that waiting is a real choice, so let's re-run the numbers and see whether structure solves this before the market gets another vote." Revisit seller credits, temporary buydowns, MI strategy, loan term, and property tax reality instead of just re-reading rate.

Required refi angle: attack dormant files through payment efficiency, not rate envy. Look for FHA borrowers who may be able to drop into conventional, borrowers carrying high-rate revolving debt who need a cash-flow reset, homeowners with enough appreciation to remove MI, and closed loans from the last 12 to 18 months that never got a true mortgage review. This is still a math-and-life-event refi market, not a broad rate-drop refi market.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell a borrower, "Let's wait for the Fed minutes because rates might improve this afternoon." That is guesswork, and it trains the borrower to treat you like a forecaster instead of an advisor. Better language is: "There are market events this afternoon, which means volatility risk is real. If today's payment solves the problem, we should respect that."

## One advanced angle

Here is the veteran angle: watch whether mortgages keep underperforming Treasuries after the 10-year auction and the Fed minutes. If the auction is soft or the minutes lean more inflation-aware, Treasuries can sell off and MBS can lag even more. That is how borrowers end up hearing, "Treasuries didn't move that much, so why did my quote worsen?" The answer is spread behavior, not just benchmark yield direction.

## Borrower talk tracks

"This is not a huge market shock, but it is a clear reminder that rates do not need a major report to move against you."

"If the payment works today, stability is useful. We do not need perfection to make a smart decision."

"A lower online quote is only better if it is actually available on your file with the same cash to close and the same certainty."

"For a refinance, I am not looking for a magic number. I am looking for a loan structure that fits your life better than the one you have now."

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a move-up buyer who paused because yesterday's payment was barely acceptable. Today's call is: "The market moved a little the wrong way, which is exactly why I wanted to reconnect now. Before you lose more ground to waiting, let's see whether a seller credit or buydown gets this payment back into your comfort zone without changing the home you want."

## One thing to watch

Watch the market's reaction after 2:00 p.m. more than the text of the minutes alone. If lenders worsen pricing late in the day after the 10-year auction and the minutes, that tells you the market is looking for excuses to stay defensive. If pricing holds together despite both events, that is a sign the sell-off may be tiring.

## Coaching close

Newer LOs: do not hide behind rate talk. Explain cause, payment, and options. Veterans: use today's mild pressure to create honest urgency without sounding theatrical. The goal is not to scare borrowers. The goal is to help them decide before the market decides for them.
