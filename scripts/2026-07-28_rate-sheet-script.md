# The Rate Sheet Script - Tuesday, July 28, 2026

## Market move

Good morning. The market tone is a little better to start Tuesday, July 28, but only a little. Mortgage News Daily still shows Monday's top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.80% because its daily survey updates around 4:00 p.m. ET, but its Tuesday morning read says MBS prices are moderately stronger and could help rates edge lower. The bigger bond-market move happened Monday, July 27, when the 10-year Treasury yield backed down to about 4.64% after trading above 4.70% on Friday as oil prices cooled and some of the inflation panic came out of the market.

## Why it matters

That matters because this is the first real sign that the market might be trying to stabilize after last week's rate shock. But stabilization is not the same thing as relief. When mortgage rates jump fast, lenders usually give back improvements slowly. So even if Tuesday opens a touch friendlier, loan officers should treat this as a fragile bounce, not an all-clear.

## What this means in plain English

Here is the plain-English version. The weather looks a little better this morning, but the ground is still wet. Borrowers may hear "bonds are up" or "rates might improve today" and think the danger has passed. It has not. We are still in a market where one headline, one Fed comment, or one hot data point can push pricing right back the other way.

## What to say to customers

What I would say is: "This morning is a little better than late last week, but we are still in a volatile market. My job is to help you make a strong payment decision, not to gamble your closing on a headline."

That line works because it sounds calm, not salesy, and it puts you back in the advisor seat.

## Lock-or-float guidance

For closings inside 15 days, the bias is still lock. For 15 to 30 days, I would still lean lock unless the borrower has real flexibility and understands the risk around the next two trading sessions. For anything beyond 30 days, floating is reasonable if the borrower can handle noise and you stay in front of it.

Today is not just about today's tone. It is about the calendar stacked behind it. Consumer confidence and home-price data hit this morning on Tuesday, July 28. Then the Fed decision lands Wednesday, July 29, at 2:00 p.m. ET, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh at 2:30 p.m. ET. Then Thursday, July 30, brings GDP and PCE-related inflation data at 8:30 a.m. ET. That is too much event risk to get casual with a near-term closing.

## Market and housing context

The backdrop is still an affordability market, not a bargain market. Freddie Mac's weekly survey had the average 30-year fixed at 6.58% as of Thursday, July 23, the highest level in nearly a year. Mortgage News Daily's daily reading is even higher, which tells you live market execution still feels tighter than the weekly average. On housing, the important question this morning is not whether home prices are collapsing. They are not. The question is whether price growth keeps cooling enough to help affordability breathe. The market goes into today's FHFA and Case-Shiller releases expecting home prices to stay sticky while buyer confidence stays soft.

On borrower sentiment, the latest hard read is still cautious. The Conference Board said June consumer confidence inched up to 91.2 from 90.6, and the University of Michigan's preliminary July sentiment reading improved to 54.4 from 49.5. That is better, but not healthy. Borrowers may be less rattled than they were in June, but they are still very payment-sensitive.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

This is a good day to remind borrowers that headline quotes are all over the map. Early Tuesday, July 28, NerdWallet showed a 30-year fixed around 6.61% APR, while Bankrate showed a 30-year fixed rate around 6.89% with a 6.97% APR at 6:30 a.m. ET. That gap is your opening.

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," say: "You probably did. The real question is whether that quote survives points, fees, lock length, credit hits, and the actual closing timeline." In a choppy market, shoppers do not just need a lower number. They need a quote that is real.

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

Today's pipeline play is a refi conversation, but not a rate-drop fantasy. Call borrowers from the last 18 to 36 months who have higher revolving debt, mortgage insurance that may now be removable, or a temporary buydown that is about to step up. The angle is monthly-cash-flow planning.

Say: "This is not a magic refi window, but it is a smart time to review whether your current setup still fits. If we can improve your monthly cash flow, remove MI, or clean up other debt, we should map it now so you are ready when the market gives us a better opening."

That keeps refinance alive without pretending rates are suddenly attractive.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

The rookie mistake is saying, "The Fed is tomorrow, so maybe just wait and rates should drop." Mortgage rates do not move in a straight line off the Fed decision. If Warsh sounds hawkish or inflation risk stays in focus, mortgage pricing can stay elevated or worsen even if the Fed does nothing.

## One advanced angle

The advanced angle is this: the market is trading two stories at once. One is the Fed. The other is inflation risk tied to oil, geopolitics, and term premium in longer bonds. That means a borrower can hear "the Fed held" and still see worse mortgage pricing if the bond market decides inflation risk is not really gone. That is a sharper explanation for sophisticated clients and agents.

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a first-time buyer under contract with a 21-day close who is already stretching to stay under a monthly payment cap. That borrower tells you they saw an online quote that looks a quarter-point better. The right answer is: "Let me test whether that quote is real with your credit, your timeline, and your cash to close. If it is not, I would rather lock something dependable than let a marketing quote put your closing at risk."

## One thing to watch

One thing to watch today is whether the bond market keeps its early gains after the 10:00 a.m. ET confidence data and whether lenders improve pricing into the afternoon instead of repricing defensively. If MBS gains hold, that suggests the market wants to stabilize. If they fade fast, last week's fear is still in control.
