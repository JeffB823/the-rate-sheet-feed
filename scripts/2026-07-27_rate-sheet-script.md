# The Rate Sheet Script - Monday, July 27, 2026

## Market move

Good morning. The setup for today is that mortgage pricing is starting the week a little calmer than Friday’s peak stress, but not meaningfully better. Mortgage News Daily’s daily survey had the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.81% on Friday, July 24, down slightly from 6.85% on Thursday, but still sitting near the worst levels of the year. In Treasuries, the 10-year yield backed off to about 4.64% on Monday morning after pushing above 4.70% on Friday as oil-driven inflation fears cooled a bit. So the headline is not relief. The headline is less panic.

## Why it matters

That matters because mortgage pricing does not need a brand-new shock to stay tough. Once the bond market reprices higher, lenders do not rush to give all of that back. A small Treasury rally helps sentiment, but until we get through the Fed meeting and the Thursday inflation and growth data, rate sheets can still stay defensive. For loan officers, this is a market where the conversation is less about chasing a perfect rate and more about managing uncertainty.

## What this means in plain English

Here is the plain-English version. Rates are still elevated, even if this morning is a little less ugly than late last week. Think of it like traffic easing from a full stop to a slow crawl. You are moving again, but you are not clear. That is the tone to bring into customer calls. Do not oversell improvement that is not really there, but do not sound panicked either.

## What to say to customers

What I would say to a customer is this: "The market got a little breathing room this morning, but we are still near the high end of the recent range. I would rather help you make a smart payment decision than try to win a guessing game on the next eighth of a percent."

That line works because it shifts the customer out of rate-shopping theater and back into outcome-based thinking.

## Lock-or-float guidance

For closings inside 15 days, the bias is still to lock. For 15 to 30 days, I would lean lock unless the borrower has strong risk tolerance and can handle volatility around Wednesday afternoon and Thursday morning. For anything beyond 30 days, floating is more defensible, but only if you set expectations clearly: this week can move the market fast in either direction.

The practical point is simple. A small improvement on Monday does not erase event risk from a Fed decision on Wednesday, July 29, at 2:00 p.m. EDT, a Fed press conference at 2:30 p.m., and GDP plus Personal Income and Outlays, which includes PCE inflation, on Thursday, July 30, at 8:30 a.m. EDT.

## Market and housing context

The bigger backdrop is still affordability pressure, not collapse. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed at 6.58% as of Thursday, July 23, the highest level in nearly a year. Housing is slowing, but not stopping. The Census Bureau reported on Friday, July 24, that June new home sales ran at a 628,000 annual pace, up 1.6% from May, with 9.3 months of supply. That tells you builders are still moving inventory, but they are doing it in a market that needs price discipline and seller help. Median new-home price also moved down to $398,300, which is another sign the market is working through affordability, not ignoring it.

On the sentiment side, the University of Michigan’s preliminary July reading improved to 54.4 from 49.5 in June, so consumers feel a little less shell-shocked than they did a month ago. But 54.4 is still weak. Borrowers are not walking in confident. They are walking in cautious and payment-sensitive.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I saw a lower rate online," the right answer today is not to argue rate for rate. The better answer is: "You probably did see a teaser. In this market, the real question is what it costs to get that number, how long it is locked, and whether it still works after fees, LLPAs, and timing pressure show up."

That works especially well right now because the spread between a headline quote and an actually executable loan can widen when markets are jumpy. Online quote shoppers are usually not just asking for a cheaper rate. They are asking for certainty without knowing how to ask for it.

## Pipeline play with refi angle

Today’s pipeline play is to stop treating refinance as a dead conversation. The angle is not "rates are low again." They are not. The angle is payment strategy and future optionality.

Call past clients who bought in the last 12 to 24 months with seller-paid buydowns, higher consumer debt, or an ARM conversation they postponed. Say: "I’m not calling because this is some magical refi window. I’m calling because markets are unstable, equity has improved for a lot of people, and I want to know whether we should map out a debt-consolidation or recast-versus-refi plan before the next real opening shows up."

That creates a real financial-planning conversation instead of a rate alert that goes nowhere.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

The rookie mistake is telling borrowers, "Just wait until after the Fed meeting and rates should come down." That is lazy and dangerous. The Fed does not set mortgage rates directly, and if the statement or press conference keeps inflation concerns alive, mortgage pricing can stay elevated or even worsen. Never promise direction when what you really mean is uncertainty.

## One advanced angle

The advanced angle is this: today’s small rally in bonds only helps if volatility keeps cooling. If oil headlines re-ignite inflation fears or if Thursday’s data runs hot, lenders can reprice defensively even without a huge move in the 10-year. Experienced LOs can use that nuance with better borrowers and referral partners: "The issue is not just where the 10-year is. The issue is whether the market trusts inflation to keep easing." That sounds sharper because it is sharper.

## Borrower talk tracks

Use these exactly if they fit your style:

- "I’m less focused on winning the internet quote contest and more focused on protecting your monthly payment from a volatile week."
- "Today feels a little better than Friday, but not better enough to gamble with a near-term closing."
- "If the market improves later, we can talk about the next move then. Right now, my job is to keep this purchase safe."
- "You do not need the perfect rate to make a good home decision. You need a payment and a plan you can live with."

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a move-up buyer closing in two weeks who is putting 15% down and already stretching to stay under a payment cap. That borrower is not a good candidate to float through Wednesday and Thursday hoping for a miracle. The right language is: "You are close enough to the finish line that protecting the deal matters more than squeezing out a maybe. Let’s lock the payment you can afford, and if the market gives us a meaningful improvement later, we will evaluate the next best step."

## One thing to watch

One thing to watch today is whether the bond market keeps its cooler tone after the open or fades back toward Friday’s fear trade. If the 10-year keeps drifting lower and Fed hike odds keep backing off from the high-30% area, the market may be telling you this week starts with stabilization. If that reverses, lenders will stay on edge fast.

## Short coaching close for the team

Your edge today is not predicting rates. Your edge is translating volatility into calm advice. Sound steady, explain the payment, and make the borrower feel like there is a plan even when the market does not feel friendly.
