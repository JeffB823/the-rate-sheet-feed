# The Rate Sheet Script

Sunday, July 12, 2026

Use Friday, July 10 market close for call guidance.

## Market move

Here is the headline for today: mortgage rates finished the week basically flat, but the bond market did not finish friendly. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed index was 6.64% on Friday, July 10, down just one basis point on the day, while the 10-year Treasury closed July 10 at 4.56% and MBS pricing weakened into the afternoon. That is not a rally. That is the market catching its breath at a higher level.

## Why it matters

That matters because a flat rate sheet can fool people into thinking risk is gone. It is not gone. The market is still carrying elevated inflation concern, and the next batch of data hits fast. When bonds feel heavy even on a quiet rate day, lenders can hold pricing steady for the moment, but they are not being invited to improve aggressively.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: this is not a "wait and rates will save us" market. It is a "make the deal work with today's payment, then stay ready to improve it later" market. Buyers did not get worse news into the weekend, but they did not get a real gift either.

## What to say to customers

Try this: "Rates did not move much going into the weekend, which is good in the sense that they did not get away from us. But I would not call this a big improvement. The win right now is making sure the payment and cash-to-close work with the market we actually have."

For the online-quote shopper: "If somebody is showing a lower headline rate, I want to compare the total cost, the lock terms, and whether that quote still works if the market gets jumpy after Tuesday's inflation report."

## Lock-or-float guidance

My read today: if the borrower is inside 15 days, lock. If they are 16 to 45 days out, floating only makes sense when the borrower has margin in DTI, extra reserves, and a clear understanding that Tuesday and Wednesday can move pricing quickly. If the deal is payment-sensitive, lock before the data. If the borrower has room and wants to take measured risk, you can discuss a float, but it should be an informed float, not a hopeful float.

## Market and housing context

Housing is still telling the same story: demand is there, but affordability is deciding who gets to act. Freddie Mac's weekly survey moved the 30-year average up to 6.49% as of July 9. Existing-home sales for June fell 2.4% from May to a 4.09 million pace, while the median existing-home price hit a record $440,600. On the construction side, May housing starts were running at a 1.177 million annual pace, down hard from April, while permits held up better at 1.413 million. That tells you builders are still willing to plan, but they are not seeing enough clean affordability to push hard on starts.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When borrowers bring you an online quote, do not take the bait and turn it into a rate cage match. Say: "A low quote is only useful if it is real, still available when you lock, and attached to a structure that actually closes on time." In this market, your edge is not pretending every quote is fake. Your edge is showing the borrower where hidden cost, relock risk, missed seller credits, or slow execution can make the cheap quote expensive.

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

The pipeline play today is two-part. First, call every active purchase borrower who is exposed to next week's data and reset expectations before the market resets them for you. Second, run a targeted refi screen, not a fantasy refi blast. Look for borrowers from the last 12 to 24 months with high note rates, expiring temporary buydowns, improved credit, enough equity to drop MI, or high-rate consumer debt that could be cleaned up. The refi angle right now is payment relief and cash-flow cleanup, not "rates crashed, everybody go."

Use this line: "I am not calling because the market suddenly got easy. I am calling because your numbers may be more improvable now than they were when you closed."

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell a borrower, "Let's wait until after CPI because rates should come down." That is not advice. That is a guess. Newer loan officers lose trust when they confuse a possible outcome with a probable one. Your job is to explain risk, not to cosplay certainty.

## One advanced angle

The advanced move is to separate note rate from borrower outcome. A veteran conversation sounds like this: "If I cannot materially improve the note rate today, can I still improve your monthly reality?" That can mean seller-paid costs, a smarter lock window, a temporary buydown, MI strategy, or debt positioning that keeps the borrower from overreaching on purchase price. In this market, execution quality is often worth more than a tiny headline rate difference.

## Borrower talk tracks

"The market is stable enough to plan, but not soft enough to assume a better rate is right around the corner."

"If the house fits and the payment fits, we can move now and keep future refinance options open."

"I do not want you shopping for a number without shopping for a full outcome."

"A cheap quote that misses the close or changes at lock is not actually the cheaper option."

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a move-up buyer who can qualify at $700,000 but feels stretched by the monthly payment. The wrong move is telling them to wait for the market to bail them out. The better move is showing two paths today: one at the top of the range that feels tight, and one maybe $35,000 to $50,000 lower with a stronger reserve position and seller help. If they choose the safer lane now, they keep control. If rates improve later, you revisit the financing from a position of strength instead of stress.

## One thing to watch

Watch the combination, not just one headline. If CPI looks softer because gas cooled but core inflation or Warsh's tone keeps the market thinking "higher for longer," mortgage pricing may not improve much. If both inflation tone and Fed tone soften together, then bonds finally have room to do better than just tread water.

## Coaching close for the team

This week, sound like the adult in the room. Calm beats clever. Explain what happened on Friday, explain what is coming on Tuesday, and help borrowers make a decision that still works even if the market does not cooperate.
