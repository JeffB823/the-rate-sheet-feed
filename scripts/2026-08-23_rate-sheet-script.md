# The Rate Sheet Script

August 23, 2026

Market move. Mortgage pricing finished the week a touch worse, not dramatically, but enough to remind us the market still does not trust the long end of the bond market. Mortgage News Daily's daily survey had the 30-year fixed at 6.77% on Friday, August 21, up one basis point from the prior day, while Freddie Mac's weekly average for August 20 came in at 6.65%, down from 6.67% the week before. That tells you the big picture is still a high-6s mortgage market, with day-to-day lender sheets moving around inside that range instead of breaking into a cleaner rally.

Why it matters. The 10-year Treasury was 4.69% on Thursday, August 20, after running as high as 4.71% earlier in the week, and that is the real story. Mortgage rates do not need a Fed hike to stay elevated. They just need bond investors to keep demanding higher yields. The Fed minutes released on August 19 reinforced that policymakers still see inflation as elevated, especially with energy pressure still in the mix, so the market is not in a hurry to price easy rate relief.

What this means in plain English. Rates are not exploding, but they are also not rolling over in a way that lets us promise a better window next week. We are in a market where small improvements can disappear fast, and borrowers who wait for a miracle are usually waiting for the wrong thing.

What to say to customers. "We are not quoting from panic, and we are not quoting from peak fear either. We are in a stubborn market. If the payment works and the house works, the smart move is to make a decision from your budget, not from a headline."

Lock-or-float guidance. For purchases closing in 30 days or less, this is still a lock-leaning market. The bond market has not earned the benefit of the doubt. For 31 to 45 days, I would call it cautiously neutral only if the borrower can absorb a modest payment change and the file has strong structure. For longer timelines, floating is really a monitored strategy, not a passive one. If the market gives you an improvement, take it instead of trying to squeeze the last eighth.

Key events and times. Monday is more about tone than data because the market is reopening after the weekend. The next cleaner housing release is New Home Sales on Tuesday, August 25, at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. The Fed calendar shows Chairman Kevin Warsh speaking from Jackson Hole on Friday, August 28, at 10:00 a.m. Eastern, and that speech matters because the bond market is still searching for conviction on how hard the Fed intends to stay on inflation. Final August consumer sentiment from the University of Michigan also lands Friday, August 28, at 10:00 a.m. Eastern.

Market and housing context. Housing is not collapsing. It is grinding. Existing-home sales in July slipped 1.7% month over month, pending home sales fell 2.3%, and July housing starts dropped 12.4%, but inventory is improving and prices are still holding up. That is a very specific kind of market: affordability is pressuring activity, but lack of a broad inventory dump is keeping the floor from falling out. For loan officers, that means urgency has to come from borrower goals and monthly payment strategy, not from pretending homes are suddenly getting cheap.

Competitive context for online quote objections. When borrowers throw an online quote at you, the mistake is debating the headline rate. The better move is to slow the conversation down. "A quote on a screen is not the same as a closed loan. The real comparison is payment, cash to close, time to close, and whether the structure still works after underwriting touches it." In this market, online shoppers are often reacting to hope more than certainty. Your job is to turn a teaser into a side-by-side decision.

Pipeline play, with the required refi angle. Do not treat this as a purchase-only market. The refi angle right now is payment strategy, not mass-rate-and-term volume. Look for FHA borrowers from the last two years who can benefit from a streamlined conversation, VA borrowers who may still improve payment stability or recapture cash flow, and conventional borrowers carrying debt who could benefit from a targeted cash-out or debt-consolidation review if the math is honest. The point is not "rates are down." The point is "let's see if your current debt stack still makes sense in today's market."

One rookie mistake to avoid. Do not tell borrowers, "Let's wait a few days and see what rates do," unless you are also defining what would trigger action. That is not advice. That is drift. If you float, attach a reason, a target, and a deadline.

One advanced angle. Mortgage spreads are still behaving better than the Treasury market would normally suggest. That is one reason mortgage rates have stayed under 7% even with the 10-year near the upper end of this year's range. Experienced LOs can use that nuance with financial clients: "Yes, bonds are under pressure, but mortgage pricing has not deteriorated one-for-one with Treasuries. That is helping keep this market more workable than the headlines make it sound."

Borrower talk tracks. "If the home fits and the payment fits, waiting for headlines to feel better is usually not a financing strategy." "You can refinance a rate later, but you cannot recover a home you lost because you hesitated through a workable approval." "I am not here to call the bottom in rates. I am here to help you make a strong decision with the market we actually have."

One concrete borrower example. Think about a move-up buyer who is approved, has stable income, and is uncomfortable because an online ad showed a rate that starts with a lower number. The right conversation is: "If I can show you the true payment, the real cash to close, and how this compares with what you can refinance into later if the market improves, would that give you enough clarity to move forward?" That keeps the borrower focused on execution instead of fantasy pricing.

One thing to watch. Watch how bonds react when the market reopens Monday morning after a week that already tested the upper end of yields. If Treasuries stay pinned near these levels and the market starts talking more aggressively about Jackson Hole, lender pricing can stay sticky even without a major new data surprise.

Coaching close for the team. Explain the market simply, advise with a trigger instead of a guess, and make every borrower conversation about payment certainty, not rate theater.
