# The Rate Sheet Script for September 2, 2026

This script was prepared at approximately 6:45 a.m. Eastern on Wednesday, September 2, 2026. The `8:15 a.m. ET` ADP report, the `10:00 a.m. ET` factory orders report, and the `2:00 p.m. ET` Fed Beige Book were not out at the time of writing, so this is a pre-data market read.

## Market move

Good morning, team. The headline today is that the rate market still has pressure on it. Mortgage News Daily closed the top-tier 30-year fixed at `6.89%` on Tuesday, September 1, up another `2` basis points on the day and at a fresh long-term high for this cycle. Mortgage-backed securities were weaker again, and the 10-year Treasury was trading around `4.80%` to `4.81%` after another ugly bond session. That means the market is starting today from a defensive posture, not a relief posture.

## Why it matters

What matters is that this is not just random lender repricing. The bond market has been selling off because higher oil is feeding inflation fear, and inflation fear is feeding higher long-term yields. Yesterday's mortgage commentary from Mortgage News Daily was blunt: more fighting, more selling. The market keeps treating war headlines and energy pressure as reasons to demand higher yields, and mortgage pricing is getting dragged along with that move.

## What this means in plain English

Here is the plain-English version. The cost of money moved up again because investors are less comfortable that inflation is cooling fast enough. Borrowers do not need the bond-market lecture. They just need to hear this: if inflation risk stays elevated, mortgage pricing stays touchy, and waiting does not automatically make the next quote better.

## What to say to customers

Say this: "Rates are higher because the bond market is still under pressure, especially with inflation worries tied to oil and the broader economy. My job is not to guess every daily move. My job is to help you make a payment decision that still works even if the market stays choppy."

If they are frustrated, say: "You are not losing because you missed one magical hour. This market has been moving in waves. What matters is whether the home, payment, and timing still make sense for your life."

## Lock-or-float guidance

This is still a lock-bias morning for anyone inside `21` days, anyone near qualification edges, and anyone whose payment works only if today's pricing holds. Protect certainty where certainty matters.

The better float case is the borrower with time, strong reserves, and enough payment cushion to absorb another small selloff. But be honest: floating into this morning means floating into event risk. That is a trade, not a strategy shortcut.

## Market and housing context

Yesterday's data gave the market a mixed but not rate-friendly backdrop. JOLTS showed job openings basically holding at `7.3 million`, so labor demand did not crack in a way that would quickly rescue bonds. ISM manufacturing stayed in expansion at `54.6`, and the prices index stayed hot at `71.1`, which is exactly the kind of inflation-sensitive detail that keeps long-term yields elevated. Construction spending for July fell `0.5%`, and residential construction spending fell `1.3%`, which tells you housing activity is not exactly roaring.

At the same time, the broader housing market is still more workable than the rate headlines make it sound. Freddie Mac said rates changed little in its weekly survey at `6.66%`, and it pointed to more homes coming on the market plus slower price growth. Redfin's current national snapshot still shows a market with more balance than the frenzy years: median price up `3.2%` year over year, sales slightly down, and supply sitting at about `4` months. That means we still have the same message: worse financing market, more negotiable housing market.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

This matters today because borrowers are shopping harder when rates push higher. ICE's 2026 Borrower Insights Survey says interest rates and lender fees still drive borrower decisions, but it also says borrowers are increasingly digital-first and service-driven. So when someone says, "I found something lower online," do not argue with the screenshot.

Say: "That online quote may be built on perfect assumptions, points, a shorter lock, or a cleaner credit profile than your actual file. I will show you the real structure on your scenario, and I will tell you where the real tradeoffs are."

Then add this: "Low headline pricing matters, but execution matters too. Cheap quotes that fall apart in underwriting or change after disclosures are not actually cheaper."

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

Today's pipeline play is segmentation with urgency. First, hit every active purchase file where another eighth in rate or a small hit to credits would change the payment conversation. Second, call the quiet pre-approvals and reposition the market: yes, rates are tougher, but seller flexibility and inventory are still better than they were when everyone was fighting over the same house.

The required refi angle is not "refinance now." It is "set the refinance review now." Tell past clients and current buyers: "If rates improve later, I want you already in my review bucket so we are not starting from scratch." In this market, the refinance win is earned by future positioning, not by forcing today's math.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Just wait for the Fed to cut and mortgage rates will drop." That is sloppy and it will make you sound uninformed when mortgage rates stay high anyway. Mortgage rates follow the bond market first, and the bond market is reacting to inflation, oil, growth data, and risk sentiment in real time.

## One advanced angle

Here is the sharper angle for veterans. The issue is not only whether the Fed hikes or pauses on September 16, 2026. The issue is that the market is demanding more compensation to own long-duration risk while inflation nerves are still active. That is why mortgage rates can stay sticky even if economic data softens at the margin. This is a term-premium and inflation-expectations story as much as a Fed story.

## One concrete borrower example

Take a first-time buyer at `$400,000` with `5%` down who was comfortable two weeks ago but is now nervous because the payment drifted higher. The wrong move is to just repeat the rate and hope they stay interested. The better move is to walk them through three choices: keep the target home and ask for seller credit, adjust the price point slightly to recover monthly comfort, or use a temporary buydown to reduce early-payment shock. That conversation turns rate anxiety into decision clarity, and that is where trust gets built.

## One thing to watch

Watch whether today's data gives the bond market any excuse to stabilize after yesterday's selloff. If ADP comes in soft, factory orders cool, or the Beige Book sounds more cautious, rates could find some footing. If the data or Fed tone keeps the inflation-and-resilience story alive, this market can stay defensive into Friday's jobs report.
