# The Rate Sheet Script for July 10, 2026

## Market move

Good morning. The market gave us a small improvement today, but not a full recovery. Mortgage News Daily said the average top-tier 30-year fixed scenario slipped about three hundredths lower to 6.50 after rates had been pressing toward 10-month highs. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey, released Thursday, July 9, showed the 30-year fixed average rising to 6.49 from 6.43 a week earlier. In Treasuries, the bond market did calm down a bit by Thursday afternoon, with the 10-year yield at 4.49 after touching 4.56 earlier in the week. So the tone is a little friendlier than yesterday, but we are still operating in an elevated-rate environment.

## Why it matters

This matters because the market is telling us two things at once. First, the bond market still wants protection against inflation and headline risk. Second, when that fear backs off even a little, mortgage rates can improve quickly. That means we are not in a clean falling-rate story. We are in a choppy, headline-sensitive market where borrowers can win a little ground and lose it again fast.

## What this means in plain English

In plain English, rates got a little better, but not better enough to start making promises. Think of it like the market taking one step back after several steps in the wrong direction. Borrowers may hear "rates improved" and assume the worst is over. That is not what this move means. It means the market paused, not that it turned.

## What to say to customers

Try this: "The market improved a little today, so pricing is a touch better than it was at the worst point this week. But we are still in a volatile range, so I would treat this as an opportunity to protect a workable payment, not as a guarantee that next week gets better."

If you need a shorter version: "Today is better than yesterday, but it is not cheap money. It is a window, not a trend."

## Lock-or-float guidance

My guidance today is slightly more balanced than yesterday, but still cautious. If the borrower is inside 15 days, payment sensitive, or tight on DTI, lock into the improvement. If the borrower has more than 30 days, strong cash, and room to absorb movement, a very cautious float is defensible, but only if they understand that Tuesday, July 14, and Wednesday, July 15, are real risk days for rates. This is not a market where I would float just because we had one decent morning.

## Key events and times

There is no major U.S. economic release scheduled for Friday, July 10, 2026. That means trading can be driven more by positioning and headlines than by a single report. The next major scheduled events are Tuesday, July 14 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern for June CPI, Wednesday, July 15 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern for June PPI, Friday, July 17 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern for import and export prices, and Friday, July 17 at 10:00 a.m. Eastern for preliminary July consumer sentiment from the University of Michigan.

## Market and housing context

Housing is still behaving like a payment-driven market. NAR said existing-home sales fell 2.4% month over month in June, but prices still hit a record median of $440,600. At the same time, Redfin said pending sales rose 1.3% week over week to the highest level in six weeks as payments briefly eased. That is the real lesson for loan officers: demand is not gone. It is highly rate-sensitive. When payments improve even a little, buyers lean back in. When rates bounce back up, they hesitate again.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "But I saw a lower rate online," do not fight the rate ad. Reframe the conversation. "You may have seen a lower headline rate, but headline rates usually assume a perfect borrower, a perfect timing window, and a cost structure that may not match your loan. I want to show you the payment, the cash to close, and the strategy, not just the prettiest number in a banner ad."

That keeps you out of commodity mode and puts you back in advisor mode.

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

Today’s pipeline play is a refi-readiness call, not a refi-victory lap. Pull the borrowers who took rates in the mid-to-high sixes, especially anyone carrying credit-card debt, FHA and VA borrowers who could benefit from a future streamline, and cash-out prospects who need payment cleanup more than headline savings today. The call is: "I am not telling you today is the magic refi day. I am telling you we should build the plan now so you are first in line when the market gives us a real window."

That same approach also protects purchase fallout. A borrower who understands the future refi path is less likely to freeze over today’s purchase rate.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Rookie mistake: overselling a small improvement. Do not tell borrowers, "Looks like rates are coming down now." That is how you lose credibility by next week. A small rally in bonds is useful. It is not a forecast.

## One advanced angle

Advanced angle: explain the difference between Treasury improvement and mortgage-rate improvement. Treasuries can rally first, while mortgage pricing lags because lenders are also watching mortgage-backed securities spreads and managing pipeline risk ahead of data. That is a smart way to explain why the 10-year can improve and the retail rate sheet may only get modestly better. Experienced borrowers and referral partners will hear that and know you understand the market beyond the headline ticker.

## Borrower talk tracks

"If the payment works today, I would rather protect a good plan than bet on a better headline next week."

"A small market improvement is something to use, not something to chase."

"The best rate strategy is not guessing perfectly. It is making sure you do not lose the house or the budget waiting for perfect."

## One concrete borrower example

Picture a move-up buyer who sold a home, has a firm contract on the next one, and is already stretching a little because taxes and insurance are higher than expected. Yesterday felt uncomfortable. Today gives that borrower a little breathing room. The right advice is not, "Let’s gamble for another eighth." The right advice is, "The market gave you a slightly better entry point. Let’s use it to protect the payment and keep the move on track."

## One thing to watch

Watch whether the 10-year Treasury can stay under the mid-4.50s and whether mortgage pricing actually follows through instead of giving back today’s improvement. If bonds hold firmer into Monday, the market may come into CPI with slightly better footing. If not, this bounce will look more like a trap than a turn.

## Coaching close for the team

Your job today is not to predict. Your job is to interpret. Tell borrowers the market improved a little, explain why that does not equal safety, and use plain language to help them make a decision before next week’s inflation data makes it for them.
