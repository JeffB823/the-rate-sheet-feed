# The Rate Sheet Script for Friday, August 14, 2026

## Market move

Good morning. Mortgage pricing heads into Friday with a slightly better tone after Thursday's bond rally. The key move was in rates, not because the market suddenly got cheap, but because inflation data stopped getting worse. Mortgage News Daily's daily survey ended Thursday with top-tier 30-year fixed pricing around 6.69%, down from 6.74% the day before, while Freddie Mac's weekly survey printed 6.67% on August 13. Treasury yields also backed off their highs, with the Fed's H.15 data showing the 10-year Treasury at 4.68% on August 12, down from 4.72% on August 10.

## Why it matters

This matters because mortgages do not need a Fed cut to improve. They need the bond market to believe inflation is cooling enough that long-term yields do not have to keep climbing. That is what happened Thursday. The move was encouraging, but it was a stabilization move, not a full reversal.

## What this means in plain English

For borrowers, this is the difference between "the market is getting away from me" and "I may have a little room to think." Payments are still high. Affordability is still tight. But the market gave buyers and refinancers a small break instead of another punch in the mouth.

## What to say to customers

"Rates improved a bit because the bond market liked the inflation data. That does not mean we are back to low rates. It means the market gave us a cleaner window to make a smart decision."

"If you are serious about buying, this is the kind of market where timing and structure matter more than chasing the lowest headline quote on the internet."

## Lock-or-float guidance

If the borrower is closing in the next two weeks, has a tight DTI, or is highly payment-sensitive, locking still makes sense. You do not get paid for being greedy in a market that can reverse on one hot report.

If the borrower is 21 to 45 days out and can handle some volatility, a cautious float is still defensible this morning, but only until the data speaks. July retail sales hits at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on Friday, August 14, 2026, and preliminary August Michigan sentiment hits at 10:00 a.m. Eastern. If either one revives inflation fears, lenders can take back Thursday's improvement fast.

## Market and housing context

The housing backdrop is still a story of strained affordability and choosy buyers. NAR reported on August 11 that July existing-home sales slipped 1.7% to a 4.06 million annual pace, with inventory at 1.54 million homes and a 4.6-month supply. Realtor.com said on August 13 that active inventory climbed 3.2% year over year to the highest level since November 2019, median list price was down 1.2% from a year ago, and price reductions stayed above 100,000 for a fifth straight week. Redfin added on August 13 that sellers outnumbered buyers by 51% in July and the number of buyers fell to a record low.

That is the real market. There are more choices. Sellers have less leverage. But buyers are still payment-capped, which means even modest rate improvement can wake up demand at the margin.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I saw a lower rate online," the answer is not to fight over the screenshot.

"That quote might be real, but the real question is what assumptions sit underneath it. Is it paying points, is it a shorter lock, is it based on a cleaner credit or equity story than yours, and does it still hold once underwriting gets involved? My job is to show you what is actually executable, not what is attractive on a landing page."

That lands well because it respects the shopper without surrendering expertise.

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

Today's pipeline play is a call-back day. Rework the buyers who paused in late July or early August when rates pushed to recent highs. Do not sell them "rates are down." Sell them "the market gave us a little relief, and we should re-test the payment."

Required refi angle: pull FHA, VA, and recent higher-coupon conventional closings from the last 60 to 120 days, especially anyone who barely made sense at the payment they accepted. This is not broad refi boom language. This is targeted triage. Look for borrowers where a small improvement, debt consolidation, or dropping MI later could turn into a real savings conversation. Plant the flag now, even if the full refi is a future move.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers that rates are "coming down" just because one day improved. One better day is a market reaction, not a trend. Say, "The market improved a bit, and now we need to see if it holds after Friday's data." That keeps your credibility intact.

## One advanced angle

The advanced read is that the Fed may be less important than the long bond for the next move in mortgage rates. The market liked cooler CPI and flat PPI, but longer-term yields are still elevated because investors are balancing softer inflation against energy risk, Treasury supply, and fiscal pressure. That means even when the Fed looks patient, mortgage rates can stay stubborn. Experienced LOs should be selling certainty, optionality, and execution quality, not promising that a Fed pause automatically leads to lower consumer rates.

## Borrower talk tracks

"A better market tone helps, but the bigger win is making sure you are ready when the numbers work."

"If the online rate is lower, let's compare the actual cost, lock, and cash to close. That is where the truth lives."

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a move-up buyer who stepped back two weeks ago because the monthly payment jumped just enough to make the math uncomfortable. Today is the day to call and say, "The market improved a little, and you may have more negotiating power on the house side too. If we pair a slightly better rate with a seller credit, the deal that felt out of reach last week may be back in range."

That is a better conversation than pretending the market changed dramatically. It shows command of both financing and housing leverage.

## One thing to watch

Watch whether Friday's retail sales and Michigan inflation-expectations components confirm the calmer inflation story. If consumers still spend hard and inflation expectations firm back up, the bond market can get defensive again quickly.

## Coaching close for the team

Be the translator today. Newer borrowers need plain English. Experienced borrowers need precision. Give both groups the same core message: the market improved a little, the opportunity is real but fragile, and your value is helping them act before a better window disappears.
