# The Rate Sheet Script for 2026-07-06

## Market move

Good morning team. Monday, July 6, 2026 starts with the bond market a little better, not dramatically better. Treasury yields are drifting lower again after Thursday's weaker June jobs report, and mortgage-backed securities are starting the week in positive territory. Mortgage News Daily shows the 10-year Treasury near 4.46% this morning and UMBS prices modestly higher, but because Friday was a market holiday, most lenders are still effectively anchored to Thursday's mortgage pricing tone until fresh rate sheets settle in today.

## Why it matters

It matters because the market is still translating one big message: growth looks slower, and that usually helps rates, but not every slower-growth signal becomes a clean mortgage rally. The June jobs report showed payroll growth of just 57,000, with unemployment at 4.2%, and that was soft enough to cool some rate-hike fear. Freddie Mac's weekly survey also moved down to 6.43% as of July 2. So the setup is friendlier than it was a few sessions ago, but not friendly enough to assume lenders will suddenly get aggressive with pricing.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: the market gave us some breathing room, not a victory lap. Buyers are not back in easy-money territory. They are just dealing with a slightly less hostile payment environment than they were before the jobs data.

## What to say to customers

Say it this way: "The market improved a little after weaker labor data, so today may be a better decision window than last week. But this is still a market where we want to act when the numbers make sense, not wait around for a perfect headline that may never show up."

## Lock-or-float guidance

For closings inside 15 days, lock. For 15 to 30 days, I still lean lock unless the borrower has real room to absorb a market reversal after this morning's data. If a file is 30-plus days out and the borrower understands risk, floating is reasonable only if you are prepared to pivot fast after the services data, Fed commentary, or Wednesday's minutes. The bias today is disciplined optimism, not casual floating.

## Market and housing context

Housing still looks payment-sensitive, not frozen. Redfin's weekly update published July 2 said the median U.S. housing payment posted its first year-over-year increase since October, while pending home sales rose only 0.4% week over week. That is the kind of market we are in: buyers are still out there, but they respond to monthly payment more than headlines. Mortgage demand tells a similar story. The MBA said applications were basically flat in its July 1 release, with purchase activity edging higher. That is not surge behavior. That is cautious borrower behavior. For our team, that means affordability conversations still matter more than rate predictions.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," do not fight the screenshot. Slow it down and say, "Let me show you whether that quote is actually lower on your file after points, lender fees, cash to close, escrows, and how reliable the approval really is." In this market, online quotes win attention because they are simple. We win trust by making the comparison real.

## Pipeline play

Today's pipeline play is to work the middle of the funnel, not just the hot leads. Reopen buyers who stepped back because last week's payment felt just a little too high. A small market improvement plus seller credits, a buydown, or a better structure can get those files moving again.

Required refi angle: do not wait for a giant rate-drop headline to start prospecting. Call the borrowers whose life changed before the market did. Think FHA borrowers who may be closer to a better conventional execution, borrowers carrying expensive revolving debt, owners who may be ready to remove MI, and anyone from the last 12 to 18 months who never got a true mortgage review after closing. The refi conversation is not "rates crashed." The refi conversation is "your current loan may no longer be the smartest version of your loan."

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Rates are going down because jobs were weak." That sounds confident, but it trains people to wait. The better language is: "Weak jobs data helped the bond market, but mortgage pricing still has to get through lender margins, Fed tone, and whatever today's data says next."

## One advanced angle

Here is the veteran angle: if a soft payroll report only gets us a modest improvement instead of a full rally, that tells you the market still does not trust the longer-term path lower. That is usually a spread story as much as a Treasury story. In plain terms, mortgage rates can lag Treasury improvement when lenders stay cautious, volatility stays elevated, or the market is still worried the Fed could sound firmer than traders want.

## Borrower talk tracks

"You do not need the perfect rate environment to make a smart move. You need a payment and a strategy that still work if the market gets choppy again."

"If the monthly number is close, this is the kind of market where structure matters. A seller credit or buydown can do more for you than waiting for a headline."

"I am not calling to promise some miracle refi wave. I am calling to see if your current loan still fits your life as well as it did when you closed."

## One concrete borrower example

Picture a move-up buyer who paused two weeks ago because the payment was just outside comfort range. Today's call is not, "Good news, rates are suddenly amazing." It is, "The market improved a bit, and that may be enough to reopen the conversation. If we pair today's market with a seller credit or a temporary buydown, we may be able to bring your payment back into range without waiting on the Fed to save the deal."

## One thing to watch

Watch whether today's ISM Services data comes in hotter than expected. If services and prices stay firm, the bond market can give back this morning's improvement quickly. If the number is softer or Waller sounds less hawkish than the market expects, lenders have room to hold or slightly improve pricing.

## Coaching close

Be the person who explains the market without making the borrower feel small. Newer LOs: stay simple and tie everything back to payment. Veterans: use today's modest improvement to create motion, not complacency.
