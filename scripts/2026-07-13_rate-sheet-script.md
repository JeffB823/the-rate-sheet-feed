# The Rate Sheet Script

Monday, July 13, 2026

Use Friday, July 10 lender pricing as the last complete mortgage-rate print, with Monday morning risk tone layered on top.

## Market move

Here is the setup for today: mortgage rates did not materially improve going into the weekend, and Monday morning is starting with fresh pressure from higher oil and firmer Treasury yields. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed index closed Friday, July 10 at 6.64%, basically unchanged on the day, but the bond market weakened late Friday and the 10-year Treasury was already back around 4.57%. This morning, oil is up again on renewed U.S.-Iran conflict headlines, and that matters because higher energy prices can quickly become an inflation problem in traders' minds.

## Why it matters

That matters because mortgage pricing does not need a terrible inflation report to get worse. Sometimes all it takes is a market that starts worrying inflation could reaccelerate. If oil is climbing, Treasury yields are climbing, and lenders were already dealing with a weaker bond market late Friday, the path of least resistance for rate sheets is defensive, not generous.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: rates are not blowing out this morning, but this is not a calm, improving backdrop either. The market is saying, "We are not ready to hand out better mortgage rates unless inflation data gives us a real reason." So the win today is certainty. If a borrower likes the house, likes the payment, and needs the transaction to work, this is a market where protecting the deal matters more than waiting around for a miracle headline.

## What to say to customers

Try this: "The mortgage market is starting the week a little cautious. Rates did not get dramatically worse, but the bond market is under pressure again because oil moved higher and the market is watching inflation closely. My job is to help you make a move that still works even if the market stays jumpy for a few more days."

For the online-quote shopper: "If someone is showing you a lower number, I want to compare the total cost, the lock period, and whether that quote still holds up after this week's inflation data. In a volatile market, the cheapest headline is not always the cheapest outcome."

## Lock-or-float guidance

My read today: if the borrower is closing in 15 days or less, lock. If they are inside 30 days and payment-sensitive, I still lean lock because tomorrow's CPI report can move the market fast. If they are 30 to 45 days out and have room in DTI, reserves, and emotional tolerance for volatility, floating can be discussed, but it should be framed as measured risk, not optimism. This is a "float only if you can afford to be wrong" setup.

## Market and housing context

Housing is still behaving like a market with demand underneath it, but not enough affordability to let that demand move freely. Freddie Mac's survey for July 9 put the 30-year fixed average at 6.49%, up from 6.43% the week before. The MBA said mortgage applications fell 2.2% in its latest weekly survey, with the refinance share slipping to 40.6% from 41.4%, which tells you borrowers are still rate-sensitive and the refi wave is not self-sustaining yet. On the resale side, NAR reported on July 9 that June existing-home sales fell 2.4% from May to a 4.09 million annual pace, even as the median price rose to $440,600. Translation: homes are still expensive enough to slow activity, but scarce enough to keep prices from cracking.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When the borrower says, "But this online lender is lower," do not get dragged into rate theater. Say: "That may be a real quote, or it may be a best-case quote. I want to compare lock terms, cash to close, points, lender fees, and execution risk." In this market, your advantage is not pretending every other quote is fake. Your advantage is showing that a shaky quote can become more expensive the moment the market moves or the file gets complicated.

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

The pipeline play today is simple. Start with purchase borrowers who are active right now and reset expectations before tomorrow's CPI. Then move to a focused refi screen. Look for borrowers from the last 18 to 24 months with note rates that still start with a 7, borrowers carrying monthly debt that could be consolidated intelligently, borrowers whose credit has improved, and borrowers who can now remove MI or shorten term without breaking payment.

The refi angle is not "rates are back, call everybody." The refi angle is "your financial position may be cleaner now even if rates are only a little better." Use this line: "I am not calling because the market got easy. I am calling because your monthly picture may be improvable now."

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers that CPI will probably help rates tomorrow. That is a forecast dressed up as guidance. A newer LO loses credibility fast when they promise direction instead of explaining risk. The right move is to say what could happen in both directions and help the borrower choose based on their tolerance, not your hope.

## One advanced angle

The advanced move is to separate market advice from payment design. A veteran conversation sounds like this: "Even if I cannot give you a materially better note rate today, I may still be able to improve your monthly reality." That can mean a better seller-credit strategy, a shorter or longer lock decision, a temporary buydown that solves the near-term payment, or a debt-payoff structure that improves usable cash flow. In this market, execution skill still beats rate obsession.

## Borrower talk tracks

"This is a market where certainty has value."

"If the home works and the payment works, we do not need a perfect rate to make a smart decision."

"I want to protect you from shopping a headline and missing the bigger cost picture."

"If rates improve later, we can revisit the financing. If the house is right now, we should not assume the market will bail us out."

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a first-time buyer approved near the top of their range who is also carrying a $450 car payment and a few thousand in card debt. The weak move is telling them to wait for rates to fall. The stronger move is showing them two plans today: one that stretches them into the max payment, and one that brings the purchase price down a notch, preserves reserves, and maybe uses seller help to keep cash to close in line. That borrower may not win with a lower rate this week. They can still win with a safer structure this week.

## One thing to watch

Watch whether tomorrow's CPI gives the bond market permission to ignore oil, or whether higher energy prices keep inflation fear in control. If CPI is soft and Warsh sounds measured, mortgage pricing can stabilize or improve. If CPI is sticky or the market thinks energy will bleed into future inflation, lenders can stay defensive even without a dramatic headline.

## Coaching close for the team

Sound calm today. Do not oversell hope, and do not oversell fear. Teach borrowers what the market is doing, explain what that means for their payment, and help them make a decision that still works if this week stays volatile.
