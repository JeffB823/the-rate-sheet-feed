# The Rate Sheet | Friday, August 21, 2026

## Market Move

Good morning team. The market is ending the week with a mixed message that matters. Freddie Mac's weekly average for the 30-year fixed moved down to 6.65% on Thursday, August 20, but Mortgage News Daily's daily index finished the same day at 6.76%, up from 6.72% on Wednesday. That gap is the story. The weekly average says rates were a little better over the full survey window. The live market says retail pricing was actually a little worse by the time lenders closed their books yesterday.

This morning, the bond market is trying to steady itself, not stage a real rally. Mortgage News Daily's dashboard showed the 10-year Treasury near 4.685% early Friday, August 21, only modestly lower on the morning. That tells you the market has not fully bought into a sustained improvement yet.

## Why It Matters

This matters because borrowers do not lock the weekly average. They lock the rate sheet in front of them. And right now that rate sheet is still dealing with three pressures at once: sticky inflation risk, elevated oil, and a bond market that still does not trust Washington's fixes to long-term yields.

Treasury did help long bonds on Wednesday, August 19 by announcing it will at least double long-end liquidity support buybacks starting September 9. That helped the 10-year Treasury fall to 4.65% on August 19 from 4.71% on August 18, according to the Fed's H.15 release. But Thursday's mortgage pricing still moved higher because the market stayed nervous about inflation and energy.

## What This Means in Plain English

Plain English: rates are not spiraling higher today, but they also have not turned the corner. The market found a little balance, not a breakthrough.

For newer LOs, here is the simple version. A weekly rate survey can look better even while today's consumer pricing gets worse. That is because weekly surveys are backward-looking averages, while your borrower is shopping the live market in real time.

## What to Say to Customers

Try this: "The market is calmer than it was earlier in the week, but not improved enough for me to call this a real rate break. We can work with today's market. We just should not oversell it."

Sharper version: "The bond market bounced, but retail mortgage pricing did not fully follow it. That usually means lenders still see enough volatility to keep some defensive margin in the rate sheet."

## Lock-or-Float Guidance

For closings inside 15 days, this is still a lock-first market. The risk of giving back a small improvement is bigger than the reward of chasing another eighth.

For 15 to 30 days, cautious float is fine only if the borrower understands that this is a management decision, not a prediction. You are floating because there is time to react, not because the market is clearly turning.

For 30-plus days, floating is still reasonable, but keep the conversation disciplined. Set a target payment, a target cost, and a check-in date. Hope is not a strategy.

Housing is still sending the same message: demand exists, but affordability is still choking conversion.

Census said July housing starts fell 12.4% from June, while single-family starts fell 9.9%, even though permits rose 5.0%. That tells you builders still want future supply, but they are pacing actual groundbreakings carefully because payment shock is still real.

## Competitive Context for Online Quote Objections

When a borrower says, "I found a lower rate online," do not fight the screenshot. Expand the frame.

Say: "Maybe that quote is real, but I need to know if it is real for you. Let's compare the rate, points, lender fees, lock period, mortgage insurance, and cash to close before we decide it is actually cheaper."

Then add: "In a volatile market, the easiest way to advertise a lower rate is to hide more of the cost in the assumptions."

## Pipeline Play

Today's pipeline play has to include the refi angle.

MBA's weekly survey on Wednesday, August 19 showed total applications down 0.4% week over week, purchase apps down 2%, and the refinance share rising to 41.9% from 40.7%. That is the tell. Even small rate improvements still wake refinance interest up faster than they fix purchase hesitation.

So work two lists today.

First, call the purchase borrowers who went quiet this month. Your message is not "great news, rates are down." Your message is "the market stopped getting worse for the moment, and that gives us a reason to rebuild your payment game plan."

Second, work the refi bench on purpose. FHA, VA, and higher-coupon conventional borrowers with monthly payment stress, heavy revolving debt, or a likely life-event in the next six to twelve months should hear from you now. Build the watchlist before the window opens, not after.

## One Rookie Mistake to Avoid

Do not tell a borrower that one decent bond day means mortgage rates are headed lower. That is how newer LOs lose credibility in a choppy market. Say what happened, not what you wish happens next.

## One Advanced Angle

The advanced angle is spread behavior. Veteran LOs know the consumer rate sheet can lag a Treasury rally when lenders think volatility is still high. That is a smart conversation with sophisticated borrowers: "I am not just watching Treasury yields. I am watching whether mortgage pricing is actually passing through the bond-market improvement."

## Borrower Talk Tracks

"The market looks more stable than it did earlier this week, but stable and cheap are not the same thing."

"My job is not to guess the perfect day. My job is to help you act when the payment works and protect you when the market is jumpy."

"If an online quote is better after we match all the details, I will tell you. But we have to compare the full deal, not the headline."

## One Concrete Borrower Example

Think about a first-time buyer who has been waiting for rates to magically come back down before making an offer. The conversation is: "I would rather help you win with today's real numbers than keep you waiting for a perfect headline that may not show up soon. If the payment works now and the house is right, we can build a refinance plan into the strategy instead of treating today's rate like a forever decision."

## One Thing to Watch

Watch whether this morning's PMI keeps the market in stabilization mode or pushes it back toward inflation fear. If traders decide growth and pricing power are still too warm, the week's small bond recovery can fade quickly.

## Coaching Close

Today's edge is disciplined translation. Newer LOs should learn the difference between a better weekly average and a better live rate sheet. Veteran LOs should use that difference to sound calmer and sharper than the internet. If you can explain the market without exaggerating it, borrowers will trust you faster.
