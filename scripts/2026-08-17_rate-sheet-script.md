# The Rate Sheet | Monday, August 17, 2026

## Market Move

Good morning team. The early tone today is a little better for rates. Treasury yields are easing, mortgage-backed securities are slightly improved, and that should translate into at least a little less pressure on mortgage pricing to start the week. This is not a big rally and it is not a victory lap kind of day. It is more of a small step in the right direction after a stretch where rates stayed stubbornly high.

## Why It Matters

What changed is not the Fed funds rate. What changed is the market’s belief about how aggressive the Fed may need to be next. Softer labor, retail, and inflation reads have taken some heat out of rate hike expectations, so the bond market is giving back a little of the fear premium it built in earlier. When bond yields drift lower, mortgage rates usually get at least some room to breathe.

## What This Means in Plain English

If you are newer, here is the simple version: mortgage rates price off the bond market, especially the 10-year Treasury and MBS, not directly off whatever headline people hear about the Fed. So when the market starts thinking the Fed may not need to get tougher, bond yields can fall even if the Fed has not cut anything. That is why a borrower can hear, "the Fed held steady," and still see mortgage pricing move the next day.

## What To Say to Customers

Try this: "We are seeing a little improvement in the bond market today, which is the part of the market that usually drives mortgage pricing. It does not mean rates suddenly got cheap, but it does mean the pressure is not moving the wrong way this morning."

And for a more experienced version: "The market is repricing Fed risk a bit lower, so mortgage pricing has some breathing room. We are not back to easy-money territory, but today is more constructive than defensive."

## Lock-or-Float Guidance

For closings inside 15 days, I would still lean lock. A small improvement is helpful, but it is not enough to justify gambling with a near-term closing, especially with fresh housing data tomorrow and Fed minutes on Wednesday.

For 15 to 30 days, this is more of a case-by-case float. If the borrower is disciplined, understands the risk, and the quote is still payment-sensitive, you can watch the next two sessions. But this is a cautious float, not a confident float.

For 30-plus days, floating is more reasonable, but only if you are willing to react quickly. The market is looking for confirmation that softer inflation and softer demand are real. If that confirmation slips, rates can back up fast.

## Market and Housing Context

Mortgage rates are still high in absolute terms even with this morning’s better tone. Freddie Mac’s latest weekly survey had the 30-year fixed at 6.67%, and Mortgage News Daily’s last daily read was higher at 6.71%. The 10-year Treasury closed last week at 4.63% after being as high as 4.72% earlier in the week, and today it is hovering a little lower again.

Housing is still affordability-first. Builder confidence has been stuck below breakeven, single-family permits and starts have been soft relative to what the market needs, and existing home sales remain constrained by the lock-in effect. Translation: supply is not loose enough to solve affordability, and rates are still high enough to keep plenty of buyers cautious.

Consumer mood is not helping either. The University of Michigan’s preliminary August sentiment reading dropped to 51.0 from 55.2 in July, and expectations weakened too. That tells you borrowers are still emotionally fragile. Even when the math works, confidence can be the missing piece.

## Competitive Context for Online Quote Objections

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," do not fight the number. Reframe the comparison.

Say this: "That may be a real headline quote, but the real question is what rate applies to your credit, your down payment, your loan size, your property type, and the cost to get it. A lot of online quotes are built to win the click, not to survive underwriting."

Then add: "If their offer is truly better, we should test it line by line. Rate, points, lender fees, escrows, lock period, and whether that quote still stands once they pull your file."

## Pipeline Play

Today’s pipeline play is a two-lane approach. On the purchase side, call every active shopper who paused in the last two weeks and give them a market update in plain English. The message is not, "rates are down." The message is, "the market pressure eased a bit, so this is a smart day to revisit options."

On the refi side, do not wait for dream rates. Refinance application volume has already been reacting to modest changes, and that is your cue. Build a "not now, but soon" list today. Focus on borrowers who took higher-rate loans in the recent spike, FHA borrowers who may benefit from streamline economics later, and cash-out prospects who can improve overall household cash flow once pricing gives them a cleaner entry point. The goal is not to promise a refi today. The goal is to own the conversation before the market gives everyone else a reason to call.

## One Rookie Mistake to Avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "The Fed minutes will decide mortgage rates." That is too simplistic and it sets you up to sound wrong. Better language is: "The minutes can move the bond market if they change expectations about future Fed policy, and that can affect mortgage rates."

## One Advanced Angle

Here is the sharper angle for veteran LOs: use the gap between borrower psychology and market reality. Consumers hear one or two softer inflation stories and assume a big rate drop is around the corner. That assumption creates hesitation. Your edge is explaining that modest bond improvement can help opportunity windows open before headline mortgage rates look dramatically different. In other words, teach borrowers to act on payment strategy, not media confirmation.

## Borrower Talk Tracks

"You do not need the perfect rate to make a smart move. You need a payment, plan, and property that still make sense if the market stays choppy."

"If the market improves more, great, we can capture that. If it doesn’t, at least you didn’t lose the house or the negotiating leverage while waiting for a headline."

"Shopping is smart. Waiting without a decision framework usually is not."

## One Concrete Borrower Example

Think about a first-time buyer at $425,000 who stepped back when rates pushed higher earlier this month. Today’s conversation is not, "Come back because rates are amazing." It is, "The market has stabilized a bit, and that gives us room to rerun the payment with seller credit, buydown structure, and a realistic lock plan." That borrower may still say no, but now the decision is based on numbers instead of fear.

## One Thing to Watch

Watch whether tomorrow’s housing data confirms cooling demand without reigniting inflation fear. If housing numbers look weak in a clean way, bonds may like it. If the market decides supply constraints are keeping shelter inflation sticky, that benefit can disappear quickly.

## Coaching Close

Today is a translator day. Borrowers do not need a rate historian. They need someone who can explain a choppy market calmly, turn uncertainty into a plan, and make the next step feel manageable. Slow the conversation down, simplify the message, and own the follow-up.
