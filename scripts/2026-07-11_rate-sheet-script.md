# The Rate Sheet Script

Saturday, July 11, 2026

Use Friday, July 10 market close for call guidance.

## Market move

Here is the headline for today: mortgage rates did not make a big move into the weekend. The daily mortgage market finished basically flat, with Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed index at 6.64%, down just one basis point from Thursday. That sounds quiet, and for borrowers it mostly is. But under the surface, mortgage bonds weakened a bit in the afternoon and the 10-year Treasury stayed elevated around 4.56. So this was not a clean rally. It was more of a pause.

## Why it matters

That matters because flat is not the same as safe. When rates hold steady while the bond market still feels heavy, lenders can keep pricing stable for the moment, but the market is telling you it is still very sensitive to next week's inflation data and Fed messaging. In other words, we did not get fresh relief. We got a breather.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: if your borrower was hoping one quiet day suddenly makes the payment easy, it does not. But if they were worried rates were running away again, that did not happen either. We are in a narrow range, not in a real downtrend. Payments are still manageable only when the full strategy is right: price point, down payment, seller help, and timing.

## What to say to customers

Try this: "Rates were basically unchanged going into the weekend. That means we still have a workable window, but I would not call this a big improvement. My job is to help you use today's market well instead of waiting for a perfect one."

For a rate shopper: "You are not missing some huge hidden drop today. The market is mostly sideways, so the bigger win is structuring the loan and the cash-to-close the right way."

## Lock-or-float guidance

My read today: if the borrower is inside 15 days, lock unless there is a very specific reason not to. If they are 16 to 45 days out, floating is only for borrowers who can handle volatility and understand that Tuesday's CPI and Chairman Kevin Warsh's testimony could move the market fast. If the deal is payment-tight, lock sooner. If the borrower has strong reserves, seller credits, and room in DTI, you have a little more flexibility.

## Market and housing context

Housing still looks rate-sensitive, not broken. Existing-home sales for June slipped 2.4% month over month to a 4.09 million pace, while the median price hit another record at $440,600. That is the exact problem borrowers feel on the phone: demand is not dead, but affordability is still doing the filtering. Redfin also showed pending sales rising to the highest level in six weeks when rates briefly eased, which tells you buyers are still there and they will move when payments improve even a little.

On the mortgage side, application activity softened during the holiday-shortened week. That is not surprising. It says the market still needs either clearer rate relief or better borrower strategy to create urgency.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "But the online lender showed me something lower," do not argue rate versus rate in a vacuum. Say: "That may be a real headline rate, but the real question is what it costs, how it is locked, how fast it closes, and whether the structure still works if the market moves before docs are out." In a sideways market, the best defense is certainty. Online quote shoppers are usually not really buying rate. They are buying the feeling that they are not overpaying. Give them transparency, not a wrestling match.

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

The pipeline play today is simple: split the list. First, every active purchase borrower gets a payment-stability check-in before Tuesday's CPI. Second, pull the closed-loan database for anyone who bought in the last 12 to 18 months with a high note rate, a temporary buydown, MI they may be able to drop soon, or enough appreciation to consolidate debt. This is not a massive rate-and-term refi wave yet. It is a targeted monthly-savings and cash-flow conversation.

Use this line: "I am not calling because rates crashed. I am calling because enough has changed in equity, debt, and market stability that it is worth running the numbers again."

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Rates are coming down, so let's wait." You do not know that, and next week is loaded with inflation risk. A newer loan officer can lose credibility fast by sounding certain in an uncertain market. Replace prediction with range-based guidance.

## One advanced angle

For stronger advisors, lean into payment control instead of note-rate obsession. In this market, the sharp move is often seller credit allocation, premium-capture strategy, short-term buydown design, or choosing when to lock around data risk. The experienced conversation is not "Can I beat that headline rate?" It is "Can I improve your total outcome without adding hidden execution risk?"

## Borrower talk tracks

"If the home fits and the payment fits, this market is good enough to act in. We can always revisit the financing later if the bond market gives us a better opening."

"The market is not rewarding delay right now. It is rewarding preparation."

"A quarter-point headline difference matters less than overpaying on points, missing a seller credit, or losing the house while waiting for a perfect rate."

## One concrete borrower example

Think about a first-time buyer who is approved up to $450,000 but really wants to stay comfortable. Instead of telling them to wait for rates to rescue the payment, show them a smarter lane now: maybe a $425,000 target, negotiated seller help, and a structure that keeps cash reserves intact. If rates improve later, great, you refinance from a position of strength. If they do not, the borrower still bought a home they can actually afford.

## One thing to watch

Watch whether Tuesday's CPI gives bonds a reason to break below this recent range or whether hotter inflation expectations keep the 10-year pinned up here. Consumer sentiment improved on July 10 as gas prices eased, but inflation expectations are still a live issue. If inflation data comes in sticky, the market can reprice fast.

## Coaching close for the team

Today is a good day to sound calm, not clever. Tell borrowers what changed, what did not, and what decision matters now. In this market, the loan officer who explains uncertainty clearly will beat the loan officer who tries to predict the future.
