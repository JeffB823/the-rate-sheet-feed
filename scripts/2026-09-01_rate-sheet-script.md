# The Rate Sheet Script for September 1, 2026

This script was prepared at approximately 6:30 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday, September 1, 2026. The 10:00 a.m. ET JOLTS report and the 10:00 a.m. ET ISM Manufacturing PMI report were not out yet at the time of writing, so this is a pre-data market read.

## Market move

Good morning, team. The headline today is simple: mortgage rates are waking up near the highest levels we have seen in more than a year. Mortgage News Daily had the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.87% on August 31, up another 6 basis points on the day, and the 10-year Treasury was around 4.79% late in the session. In plain terms, bond prices got hit, mortgage-backed securities weakened, and consumer rate quotes got worse.

## Why it matters

What matters is not just that rates are higher. It is why they moved. Yesterday's move looked like a combination of heavy month-end bond selling, higher oil, and a market that is still reacting to Chairman Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole message. When the market starts believing inflation could stay sticky and the Fed could stay tougher for longer, long-term yields rise first, and mortgage rates usually follow.

## What this means in plain English

Here is the plain-English version you can give a borrower: money got more expensive again. Not because their file changed. Not because the lender woke up in a bad mood. The bond market decided future inflation and future borrowing costs still look uncomfortably high, so mortgage pricing had to adjust.

## What to say to customers

Say this: "Rates moved higher because the bond market sold off, and mortgage pricing follows that market every day. The bigger point for you is not trying to win every eighth of a percent. It is making sure the home, payment, and strategy still work if the market stays jumpy for a few more days."

If the borrower is anxious, say: "A quote is a snapshot, not a promise. In volatile weeks, we make decisions based on your payment tolerance and timeline, not on hope."

## Lock-or-float guidance

Today is a lock-bias morning, especially for borrowers who are inside 21 days, for shoppers at the edge of qualification, and for anyone who would be emotionally rattled by another market selloff. If a borrower needs the current payment to work, protect the win.

The only cleaner float case is the borrower with time, pricing cushion, and a realistic understanding that we have two major data points at 10:00 a.m. ET today plus a full jobs week ahead. Floating right now is a volatility decision, not a confidence decision.

## Market and housing context

Housing is not dead. It is slower and more negotiable. Redfin's latest weekly data showed new listings up 0.4% week over week and total homes for sale up 0.5% week over week through August 23, reaching the highest level since May, while demand slipped. HousingWire's tracker also described demand as slowed but still stable, with more inventory and more price cuts.

That is the lane for today's conversations. Higher rates are real, but buyers who stay active are getting a better selection and more room to negotiate than they had in tighter inventory periods. This is not a great rate market, but it is a more workable house market.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "I saw something lower online," do not argue with the screenshot. Reframe the comparison.

Say: "You may have seen a teaser quote built around perfect assumptions, discount points, or a different lock window. My job is to show you the real cost on your actual scenario and help you decide when to act."

Then move from rate to certainty: "A cheap screenshot does not close a loan. A tight strategy, fast communication, and no surprises at conditional approval do."

## Pipeline play with required refi angle

Your pipeline play today is not waiting for a magical rate drop. It is segmentation.

Call every purchase borrower who is payment-sensitive and reset expectations early. Call every pre-approval that has gone quiet and reposition today's market as a negotiation market, not just a rate market. Then pull your closed-loan database for cash-out debt consolidation, higher-rate FHA-to-conventional, and recent buyers who need a future refinance game plan.

The refi angle is this: in a choppy market, a refinance conversation does not start with "rates are better today." It starts with "when your break-even window opens, I already want the plan built." The win is booking the review now, not forcing the refinance today.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "The Fed sets mortgage rates." That is lazy language and it creates confusion the moment market pricing moves without a Fed meeting. The cleaner explanation is: "Mortgage rates are driven mainly by the bond market, especially mortgage-backed securities and Treasury yields, and Fed policy shapes that backdrop."

## One advanced angle

Here is the sharper angle for veterans: higher rates are not the whole story. The bigger issue is term premium. The market is demanding more compensation to hold longer-duration bonds, and that can keep mortgage rates elevated even if short-term Fed expectations cool a bit. That is why rate relief can feel slower than borrowers expect.

## Borrower talk tracks

Use these as written if you want.

"If the payment still fits your budget and the house still fits your life, today's question is whether you want certainty or more market risk."

"This market rewards prepared buyers. You may not love the rate, but you may love the leverage you have with sellers."

"We can marry the house and date the rate, but only if we make sure you can comfortably afford the payment before that future refinance ever shows up."

"You do not need the perfect market. You need a plan that still works in an imperfect one."

## One concrete borrower example

Take a borrower buying at $425,000 with 10% down who was barely comfortable last week. This week, the rate move may push the payment enough to matter. That does not automatically kill the deal. It means you rework the structure: ask for seller credit, compare a temporary buydown versus permanent points, tighten insurance and tax assumptions, and confirm the real monthly comfort zone before they offer. The value you bring is not quoting faster. It is keeping a live deal alive with better structure.

## One thing to watch

Watch the market reaction after 10:00 a.m. ET today more than the headline number itself. If JOLTS and ISM come in hot, the market can read that as more fuel for higher yields. If they cool meaningfully, rates could stabilize, but this is still jobs week, so any improvement may be fragile.

## One short coaching close for the team

Sound calm today. Borrowers do not need a rate historian. They need an adult in the room. Explain the move clearly, give them a decision framework, and turn volatility into trust.
