# The Rate Sheet Script for 2026-07-07

## Market move

Good morning team. Tuesday, July 7, 2026 is starting with mortgage pricing basically steady, not materially better. Mortgage News Daily's daily survey finished Monday with the top-tier 30-year fixed at 6.59%, just 1 basis point lower than the prior update, while early Tuesday Treasury trading shows yields edging higher again, with the 10-year near 4.49% and the 2-year near 4.14%. That is a "market is waiting" tone, not a breakout tone.

## Why it matters

It matters because when mortgage rates hold mostly flat while Treasury yields firm up a little, lenders are telling you they are not eager to pass through a pricing gift until the next real catalyst shows up. This week has two obvious ones: Treasury supply starting with the 3-year auction today and the 10-year reopening tomorrow, plus the June 16-17 FOMC minutes at 2:00 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday. In plain terms, the market is calm because it is bracing.

## What this means in plain English

Plain English: rates did not get worse in a meaningful way, but they did not get easier either. We are still in that same six-and-a-half-ish mortgage world where a borrower can win with the right structure, but waiting for a miracle headline is usually not the smartest strategy.

## What to say to customers

Say it this way: "The market is steady today. That's helpful, because it lets us make a clean decision without chasing a panic move. If the payment works, we should treat stability as an opportunity, not sit around hoping the Fed hands us a gift."

## Lock-or-float guidance

For closings inside 15 days, lock. For 15 to 30 days, I still lean lock because tomorrow afternoon's Fed minutes can move bonds quickly even if the headlines look subtle. For 30-plus days, floating is reasonable only when the borrower understands that this week includes both Fed language risk and Treasury auction risk. The bias today is not fear, but it is definitely not casual floating.

## Market and housing context

Housing still looks active, but extremely payment sensitive. Redfin's July 2 weekly update said the median U.S. housing payment posted its first year-over-year increase since October, even as pending sales still inched up week over week. Realtor.com's June housing report said asking prices fell 2.5% from a year earlier, pending sales rose for a seventh straight month, and price cuts climbed to 18.8% as the market moved into summer. That is a useful message for borrowers: activity is not frozen, but affordability pressure is forcing more realism from sellers.

Borrower sentiment also still looks cautious underneath the surface. The latest New York Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations, released June 8, said households' outlooks on their finances and credit access deteriorated, even while home-price growth expectations moved up. That means many borrowers still want a home, but they do not feel loose or confident with monthly cash flow. Your conversation has to respect that.

## Competitive context for online quote objections

When a borrower says, "The online lender showed me something lower," do not try to win the argument with another headline rate. Say, "Maybe they did. Let's compare what that quote actually costs in points, lender fees, cash to close, escrows, and how reliable the approval is on your exact file." In a stable-but-expensive rate market, the internet wins with simplicity. We win by translating the fine print into monthly reality.

## Pipeline play

Today's pipeline play is to rework paused buyers who were close, not cold. This is a strong day to call the borrower who was fifty to one hundred dollars outside their comfort zone and revisit structure: seller credits, temporary buydowns, different down payment options, or a slightly different target price.

Required refi angle: run mortgage reviews, not rate-drop pitches. The best refi calls right now are not "good news, rates crashed." They are "your current loan may no longer be your best loan." Think borrowers with removable MI, FHA-to-conventional candidates, cash-out borrowers using high-rate consumer debt, and 2024 to 2025 closings that never got a real post-close review. In this market, refi opportunity comes from math and life changes before it comes from a giant rally.

## One rookie mistake to avoid

Do not tell borrowers, "Rates should improve after the Fed minutes." You do not know that, and it encourages waiting. Better language is: "Tomorrow can move the bond market in either direction, so if today's numbers already solve the problem, we should respect that."

## One advanced angle

Here is the veteran angle: if the 10-year Treasury stays near the high side of its recent range and retail mortgage pricing still refuses to worsen much, that tells you lenders are guarding margin but not seeing immediate fallout risk. If auction demand is weak this week or the Fed minutes read more inflation-sensitive than the market wants, mortgage spreads can widen again and erase the benefit of any Treasury rally. In other words, do not watch Treasuries alone. Watch how well mortgages keep up with them.

## Borrower talk tracks

"A stable market is not a bad market if the payment works. Stability gives us a chance to make a smart decision without rushing."

"I would rather help you control the payment with structure than ask you to gamble on a headline."

"If another lender is advertising lower, let's test whether it is truly lower on your file or just cleaner-looking marketing."

"For a refinance, the question is not whether rates are amazing. The question is whether your current mortgage is still the right fit for your life and cash flow."

## One concrete borrower example

Picture a first-time buyer who paused over the weekend because the payment felt just a little too high at last week's numbers. Today's call is: "The market has held steady, which means we can stop guessing and model the real options. If we pair today's rate with a seller credit for a buydown, we may be able to turn a near-miss into an approval range you can actually live with."

## One thing to watch

Watch tomorrow's Fed minutes less for the obvious rate decision, which we already know, and more for the tone around inflation and how uneasy the Committee sounded about cutting later this year. If the minutes feel more hawkish than the market expects, lenders can reprice worse quickly even without a shocking economic report.

## Coaching close

Teach the borrower what the market means, then tell them what to do with it. Newer LOs: keep bringing the conversation back to payment and options. Veterans: use this stable window to create decisions before the next volatility window hits.
